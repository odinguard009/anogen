# Chapter 5: The Thing in the Circle

The Undercroft maze had no lamps in its passages. Pale stones were set into the floor every ten steps, and their light came up from below, so faces looked like masks and the walls were never quite there. Four passages crossed at the middle of the maze, and a circle of smooth stone had been swept clean at the crossing. A smell of wet lime clung to everything down there, even to the straps on a bag.

Eden came down the south stair with the others. Felix walked beside him with a bow across his chest, and he kept running his thumb along the string. The bow was plain black wood with a short pull and a leather grip, made for a fast shot in a wood, not for a room of stone. He did not draw it. He only checked the string twice and looked at the circle.

Okoru stood at the south gate with his one arm behind his back. "Field drill," he said. "One construct, one student, three minutes."

"Why just one?" Rowan asked.

"Because four of you would break it in ten seconds."

"That is a good thing," Felix said.

"It belongs to someone," Okoru said. "That is the bad part."

He pointed into the box with his stick. "What is wrong with it?"

"Everything," Rowan said.

"More useful than that."

Felix leaned on his bow. "The tag says thirty per cent."

"And it walks as if it is angry," Clara said.

"The wire is cut," Eden said.

"Say the rest."

"It was cut from this side. The ends are clean."

Okoru looked at him for a beat. "Then we are all watching the same thing."

The four passages had names cut into the stone at the crossing, and the letters had been worn smooth by boots. The false walls ran on brass wheels that someone oiled too well. The one that would close on Lysette had made no sound until it was already moving.

Rowan shifted his spear from one hand to the other. "I'll go first."

"No," Lysette said.

"You went first last time."

"I won last time."

"That is not a reason."

"It is today." She walked past him into the passage, and Rowan let her go with his mouth shut.

Clara touched his sleeve. "You hit her arm yesterday."

"I know."

"Then let her have this one."

Okoru pointed with his stick, and the hall's small noise died. Rowan went west with Clara, and their boots faded into the passage. Rowan's spear scraped once on a corner. Clara's posting ribbon showed pale for a moment and then was gone.

Eden stayed at the gate a breath longer than the others. He looked at the north wall, where the false walls waited in their slots. Three of the four had a dry grey line of old grease along the track. The fourth, the one above the north passage, had a bright wet line, fresh enough to catch the floor light. Someone had oiled that one today. Nobody oils a wall they only mean to leave alone.

He did not say it. A thing said in front of Okoru became a thing Okoru wrote down, and a thing Okoru wrote down became a name on a list. Eden filed it with the others, in the box where he kept what was none of his business, and then he looked at the box again and found it was getting full.

Felix stood beside the gate. "I can shoot through the gap if the wall slides."

"You can shoot a wooden blade," Okoru said. "It will not care."

"I know. I like the string."

"Keep it ready."

Lysette stepped into the circle alone. She had her batons out, left one tilted higher, and she nodded once to Okoru.

"Begin," he said.

* * *

It came out of the south wall, behind Eden, and walked past him into the circle.

The construct was cut from grey stone and stood as tall as a man, with brass rings at the shoulders and knees and a wooden practice blade in its right hand. Two red beads sat in glass in its face. A leather tag hung from its wrist with THIRTY PER CENT written on it in black. The wire that should have held that limit had been cut, and the cut end swung loose as it moved.

"Thirty per cent," Felix said from the gate.

Eden watched it cross the circle. "It is not running at thirty per cent."

"I was afraid you would say that."

Lysette stood in the middle of the circle, ten steps from the gate. The north wall was four steps behind her. Rowan and Clara had gone round by the west passage and were somewhere beyond the north wall. A drill construct moved like a tired man doing a job. This one crossed the circle in three long steps with its blade already high.

Lysette went in under the blade. Her left baton caught the wooden edge, and her right came across into the brass ring at the elbow. The ring bent. The arm hung loose, but the stone fingers still held the wooden blade, and it swung from the hanging arm like a flail.

"One," she said.

Lysette stepped inside the swing, where the short reach of her batons was an advantage, and struck the other arm at the shoulder. Stone cracked. The arm came away at the brass joint and slid across the floor into the dark.

"Two."

Two arms broken, and it was still coming. She was winning, and everyone at the gate could see it.

Then it used its head. The glass face drove into her chest and pushed her back, one step, two steps, four steps, until her shoulders hit the north wall. A grind of stone filled the maze. Above her, the oiled wall dropped out of its slot and slid across the mouth of the north passage, the only way out behind her. It shut with a bang.

"Lysette!" Rowan's voice came through the stone.

"I can hear you," she said.

"Can you get out?"

"I am busy."

On the far side, Rowan put his spear into the seam of the wall and bore down. It did not move. Clara set her hand flat on the stone. "It came down when she had her back to it. Someone set it to close."

"Who?"

"Someone who knew the drill."

Lysette struck the glass face with both batons. The core sat behind a doubled plate of brass, and the plate did not move. One of her batons split down the middle and fell in two pieces at her feet. She kept fighting with the other.

"Two minutes," Okoru said.

From the gate, Felix lifted the bow and lowered it. "No angle. She is in the way."

"I know," Okoru said.

"Open the wall," Rowan said through the stone.

"It opens at three minutes."

"She does not have three minutes."

"I know."

The construct pushed again. Its head went into Lysette's shoulder, and her feet slid. She brought the last baton down on the glass, and the glass held. Her breath was loud enough to hear from the gate.

Under the pale floor light, the construct threw a long shadow up the north wall. The hanging arm was rising again with the wooden blade in its fist, and Lysette had nowhere left to step.

* * *

Eden walked into the edge of the light, six steps from the wall, and reached into his own shadow for **[Exa]**. The lance came out of the dark under his hand, cold and matte, with its iron ring-knocker, a little longer than his arm. He did not put himself between them. He put himself beside the arm, on its left.

The wooden blade came down at Lysette's head.

Eden knocked once on the ring, and **[Exa]** folded with a sound like a box lid shutting into the tray-sized door-shield. The black shield met the blade in the air. The ring closed around it like a hand. That was **[Latch]**: the ring held the blade, and Eden steered it where he wanted. His arm took the whole weight. His fingers went numb around the strap.

He steered the blade, and the arm on it, down and to the left, until they lay across the construct's shadow on the north wall. He knocked twice on the stone.

The shadow on the wall split open into a black doorway, one side of it lined with thin white light. The blade and the arm went into it. Eden shut the Door on them, and the stone arm stayed there, held in a place that had no room for it.

The construct lurched against its own trapped arm. For half a second the brass joint at its shoulder stood open.

Eden drove the shield's edge into the joint.

Something cracked that was not stone. The red eyes went out, first the left, then the right. The construct went down on its knees and then flat, with its broken arm still held in the dark.

Lysette stared at the place where the opening had been. "Where did it go?"

"Nowhere," Eden said.

"That is not an answer."

"It is the only one."

The shield unfolded back into **[Exa]** with a ring and a cold ache behind Eden's eyes. He put the lance away. His arm felt as if it had been struck by a hammer, and he flexed the fingers until they answered.

Lysette stood against the wall with one whole baton and one empty hand.

The false wall slid open. Rowan came through first with his spear low, and Clara behind him. Okoru walked in from the south gate with his wooden stick.

Clara came to Eden first, not to Lysette. She took his left wrist and turned the hand over, the way a woman checks a loaf. The fingers were still white to the second knuckle.

"Numb?" she asked.

"A little."

"How little?"

"I can hold a spoon."

"That is not the test." She pressed two fingers into the heel of his palm, and a warm thread ran up into the wrist and stopped at the elbow, as if it had met a closed door. Clara frowned at her own hand. "Odd. It does not want to go further."

"It is only a bruise."

"It is a strange bruise." But she let go, because Lysette was watching, and Clara knew when a question had to wait for a better room.

"Two seconds," Lysette said. "Again."

Eden said nothing.

She looked at his arm, then at the thing on the floor. "I didn't need that."

"I know."

Her hands shook once. She put them behind her back before anyone else could see.

"You were supposed to take the wall apart," Rowan said to Okoru.

"You were supposed to hold three minutes," Okoru said.

Rowan looked at Lysette's split baton. "I did that to you yesterday."

"Today as well," she said.

"I know."

Clara went to her and took the arm without asking. Lysette let her, which surprised them both. "It is a scratch."

"It is the same scratch," Clara said. "It has had a busy day."

Felix came up behind them with the bow still across his chest. "I never shot."

"No," Eden said.

"I had the string ready."

"I know."

Okoru was not listening. He crouched by the construct and set his stick across his knees. "Three minutes," he said. "She held it three minutes."

Nobody answered him. The pale stones hummed under the floor, and the small hall went quiet in the way a room goes quiet when someone has found a thing that should not be there.

Okoru rose and came to stand in front of Eden. "The shield."

"Sir."

"That hold again. Not by feel."

"It was a drill."

"That was not a drill." He looked at the construct, then at Eden's arm. "Somebody taught you to catch a blade like that."

"Nobody taught me anything," Eden said.

"Somebody did."

He turned away before Eden could answer, and Lysette watched him go with her one whole baton held across her body.

"Who signed the order?" she asked.

"That is not today's lesson," Okoru said.

"I will ask the office."

"You will not like the answer."

"Was the tag cut here?"

He did not answer. He moved the stick aside and put a finger on the broken plate.

Eden knelt to look. The cracked plate had opened along the shield's cut. Lysette crouched beside him and stopped.

"That is not in the drill," she said.

"No," Okoru said.

Inside, where glass should have been, there was grey dust packed so tight that it kept the shape of the plate.
