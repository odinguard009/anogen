# Chapter 7: The Warrant

Morning. Eden woke to a knock. He opened the door. A man in a dark coat stood there. He held a paper.

"Warrant," the man said. "For Eden Wren. To appear before the registrar."

Eden took the paper. It said: "Absences. 61. Unexcused. Penalty: loss of scholarship. Hearing: Friday."

Eden looked at the man. "Who sent this?"

"The registrar," the man said. "By order of the headmistress."

Eden closed the door. He looked at the paper. 61 absences. He had counted. He knew.

He went to breakfast. Lysette was there. She saw his face. "What happened?"

"Warrant," Eden said. "Absences."

Lysette took the paper. She read it. "61. You have 61 absences."

"I know."

"You cannot fight a warrant with a lance," Lysette said.

"I know."

Lysette sat down. She thought. "I will come with you. Friday."

"You do not have to."

"I know. I will come."

Eden ate. He did not taste the food. He thought about the warrant. Someone had reported him. Someone was watching his absences. That meant someone was watching him.

After breakfast, he went to the registrar's office. The registrar was a thin man with glasses. He sat behind a desk. Papers everywhere.

"Eden Wren," the registrar said. "61 absences. You are here to explain."

"I was sick," Eden said.

"You were not sick. You were on the roof."

Eden did not answer. The registrar was right. He had been on the roof. But he could not say that.

"Friday," the registrar said. "The headmistress will decide."

Eden left. He walked to the roof garden. He sat on the bench. He looked at the city. The warrant was a problem. Not a big problem. But a problem. Someone was pushing him. Someone wanted him noticed.

He thought about who. The clerk at the exchange. The Veil. The warrant. Maybe they were connected. Maybe not.

He pulled out his notebook. He wrote: "Warrant. 61 absences. Friday. Who reported?"

He closed the notebook. He would find out. He always found out.

Felix found him on the roof. "Eaten today?" Felix asked.

"Technically," Eden said.

Felix sat beside him. "The warrant. I heard."

"Everyone heard," Eden said.

Felix was quiet. Then he said, "I can talk to my father. He knows the registrar."

"No," Eden said. "I will handle it."

Felix nodded. He did not push. That was why Eden liked him.

They sat in silence. The wind moved the plants. The city hummed.

Eden thought about Friday. He would go. He would stand. He would say little. The headmistress would decide. He would accept whatever she said.

But he would also watch. Who was there. Who was watching him. He would remember faces.

He always remembered faces.
