# Chapter 7: The Warrant

Morning came with a knock on Eden's door. He opened it, and a man in a dark coat stood there, holding a paper.

"Warrant," the man said. "For Eden Wren. To appear before the registrar."

Eden took the paper and read it carefully. "Absences. Sixty-one. Unexcused. Penalty: loss of scholarship. Hearing: Friday."

Eden looked at the man. "Who sent this?"

"The registrar," the man said. "By order of the headmistress."

Eden closed the door. He looked at the paper again. Sixty-one absences. He had counted them himself. He knew every one.

He went to breakfast. Lysette was there, and she saw his face. "What happened?" she asked.

"Warrant," Eden said. "Absences."

Lysette took the paper and read it. "Sixty-one. You have sixty-one absences."

"I know."

"You cannot fight a warrant with a lance," Lysette said.

"I know."

Lysette sat down and thought for a moment. "I will come with you. Friday."

"You do not have to."

"I know. I will come."

Eden ate. He did not taste the food. He thought about the warrant. Someone had reported him. Someone was watching his absences, which meant someone was watching him. He needed to find out who and why before Friday.

After breakfast, he went to the registrar's office. The registrar was a thin man with glasses, and he sat behind a desk covered with papers.

"Eden Wren," the registrar said. "Sixty-one absences. You are here to explain."

"I was sick," Eden said.

"You were not sick. You were on the roof."

Eden did not answer. The registrar was right. He had been on the roof. But he could not say that. He could not say what he did on the roof. He could not say anything about the Veils, the lamps, or the Thirteenth Vigil.

"Friday," the registrar said. "The headmistress will decide."

Eden left. He walked to the roof garden and sat on the bench. He looked at the city. The warrant was a problem. Not a big problem, but a problem. Someone was pushing him. Someone wanted him noticed.

He thought about who. The clerk at the exchange. The Veil. The warrant. Maybe they were connected. Maybe not. He needed more information.

He pulled out his notebook and wrote: "Warrant. Sixty-one absences. Friday. Who reported?"

He closed the notebook. He would find out. He always found out.

Felix found him on the roof. "Eaten today?" Felix asked.

"Technically," Eden said.

Felix sat beside him. "The warrant. I heard."

"Everyone heard," Eden said.

Felix was quiet. Then he said, "I can talk to my father. He knows the registrar."

"No," Eden said. "I will handle it."

Felix nodded. He did not push. That was why Eden liked him.

They sat in silence. The wind moved the plants. The city hummed below them.

Eden thought about Friday. He would go. He would stand. He would say little. The headmistress would decide. He would accept whatever she said.

But he would also watch. Who was there. Who was watching him. He would remember faces.

He always remembered faces.

---

The next morning, Eden went to the registrar's office again. The registrar was not there. A different clerk sat at the desk instead.

"Can I help you?" the clerk asked.

"I want to see the warrant file," Eden said.

The clerk looked at him. "I cannot show you that."

"I am the student named in the warrant. I have a right to see it."

The clerk shook his head. "The registrar keeps those files. He is out today."

Eden looked at the desk. There were papers everywhere. Files. Folders. He could not find the warrant file among them.

"When will the registrar be back?" Eden asked.

"Tomorrow," the clerk said.

Eden left. He walked to the roof garden. Lysette was there, sitting on the bench with her notebook.

"I went to the registrar," Eden said. "He was not there."

"What did you find?" Lysette asked.

"Nothing. The clerk would not show me the file."

Lysette wrote in her notebook. "I will try. I am on the Discipline Committee. I have access to student records."

"Will that work?"

"It might. I will go this afternoon."

Eden sat beside her. "Thank you, Lysette."

"You do not have to thank me. I want to help."

Eden looked at the city. The lamps were on. The trams were running. The city was normal. But Eden knew it was not normal. The clerk at the exchange. The Veil. The warrant. Something was happening.

"Lysette," Eden said. "The Veil in the exchange. It spoke. That is not normal."

Lysette put down her notebook. "I know. I was there. It spoke. It answered you."

"What did you see? With Witness?"

Lysette was quiet. Then she said, "I saw the Veil. Before you killed it. It was standing at the switchboard. It was not doing anything. It was just standing there. Waiting."

"Waiting for what?"

"For us. For you. It knew you were coming."

Eden looked at her. "How could it know?"

"I do not know. But it knew. It was waiting. It did not try to run. It did not try to fight. It waited."

Eden thought about this. A Veil that waited. A Veil that spoke. A Veil that was not normal.

"Lysette," Eden said. "I need to check the exchange again. The clerk. I need to know what he knows."

"I will come with you," Lysette said.

"No. I go alone."

"You always go alone."

"Yes."

Lysette looked at him. She did not argue. She knew he would not change his mind.

"Be careful," she said.

"I am always careful," Eden said.

He left and went to the exchange. It was afternoon. The clerk was at his desk. He looked up when Eden entered.

"Can I help you?" the clerk asked.

"I want to ask you something," Eden said.

The clerk looked nervous. "What?"

"The Veil. Upstairs. Did you know she was there?"

The clerk's face went white. "I... I do not know what you mean."

"You do. You let us pass. You smiled. You knew."

The clerk stood. "I do not know anything. I am just a clerk. Please leave."

Eden looked at him. The clerk was scared. Not guilty. Scared. There was a difference.

"Who are you scared of?" Eden asked.

The clerk did not answer. He sat down and put his head in his hands.

Eden left. He stood outside the exchange. He thought about the clerk. Scared. Not guilty. Scared of someone else.

The Ashen Crowns. The teacher. Someone else.

Eden would find out. He always found out.

He went home and sat on his bed. He opened his notebook and wrote: "Veil spoke. Not normal. Book says Veils do not think. Archivist says new. Clerk scared. Not guilty. Scared of someone. Find who."

He closed the notebook and lay back. He stared at the ceiling.

The crack in the corner looked like a river. It always looked like a river.

He slept. No dreams.

---

The next day, Eden went to the arms yard. He trained with his lance. He practiced the Latch. The shield catches a blade and locks it against the knocker ring. He had learned this form from the Tutor, in a dream.

After training, he went to the dining hall. He sat with Felix. Felix pushed a piece of bread across the table. Eden took it and ate. The bread was stale. He did not mind.

"You look tired," Felix said.

"I am fine," Eden said.

"You are always fine. That is the problem. You are never not fine, even when you are not fine."

Eden ate the bread. He did not answer. Felix was right. But Eden did not know how to be not fine. He did not know how to say it. He did not know how to feel it.

He knew facts. He knew plans. He knew numbers. He did not know feelings. Feelings were for other people.

After breakfast, Eden went to the library. He looked for books about Veils. He found one. It was old. The cover was torn. The title was "On the Nature of the Unborn."

He opened it. The pages were yellow. The writing was small. He read.

"Veils are not born. They are made. They are shaped from Hush matter. They are given a face. They are given a name. They are given a purpose. They are tools. They do not think. They do not feel. They follow orders."

Eden read this again. "They do not think. They do not feel." But the Veil in the exchange had spoken. She had answered Eden's question. She had been aware.

Either the book was wrong, or the Veil was not a normal Veil.

Eden closed the book and put it back. He would check the archives. The archivist might know more.

He went to the basement. The archivist was there, surrounded by books. He was small. He had a beard. He wore a coat with many pockets.

"Archivist," Eden said. "I need to know about Veils. The book says they do not think. But the one I killed spoke."

The archivist looked at him. "You killed a Veil?"

"Yes."

"And it spoke?"

"Yes. It said, 'I do not have one.' When I asked for its middle name."

The archivist was quiet. He took off his glasses, cleaned them, and put them back on.

"That is not normal," the archivist said. "Veils do not speak. They do not answer. They do not have words. If it spoke, it was not a normal Veil."

"What was it?"

"I do not know. But it was something else. Something new. Or something old. Something we have not seen before."

Eden thought about this. A Veil that spoke. A Veil that was aware. A Veil that was not normal.

He would find out. He always found out.

He left the basement and went to the roof garden. Lysette was there, writing in her notebook. She looked up.

"Eden," she said.

"Lysette."

She looked at him. Her blue-violet eyes were steady. "You are worried."

"I am not worried. I am thinking."

"About what?"

"The Veil. It spoke. That is not normal."

Lysette put down her notebook. "I know. I was there. It spoke. It answered you."
