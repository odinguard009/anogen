# Chapter 11: The Direction of Shadows

The maze had lamps for the graded run, small iron lamps fixed at shoulder height every twelve steps. Each one threw a hard shadow along the floor. The passage smelled of wet stone and lamp oil. The lamps had been set the night before by someone who knew exactly where the false walls would be. Okoru stood under the first lamp with a board in his hand and a stopwatch hanging from his one wrist.

"Year 5 spec," he said. "False walls, two timed constructs, one exit. Your Lance goes in together and comes out together. I mark the time, the touches you take, and whether you listen to each other."

Rowan rolled his shoulders. "Lance One went first."

"They went first and came out in nine minutes." Okoru looked at each of them in turn. "They are very good at being five people who already agree."

"And we are not," Lysette said.

"You are five people who have never worked as one unit." Okoru turned the board so they could see the columns. "That is not an insult. It is the thing I can see from here."

Felix read the columns upside down. "Which column says we lose?"

"There is no column for losing. There is a column for order."

Eden looked at the lamps. Their shadows all lay across the passage, except one, which ran along it toward the north corner. He put that away without saying anything. A graded run was not a Vigil job. He did not need to be the one who found the exit.

"That last column is the one that matters," Okoru said.

"Why?" Felix asked.

"Because I have watched the top Lance work. They are fast because they already know what the others will do. You are fast when you stop being surprised by each other."

"How do we do that?" Rowan asked.

"Listen."

"That is all?"

"That is all." Rowan looked at his own hands. "So I should not shout every time I hit something."

"That would be a start," Okoru said. "But do not stop hitting things."

Behind them the other Lances waited at their own lamps. Valerius stood with his Lance and did not look over, which was how the others knew his run had started as well.

"Lance Nine," Okoru said. "Go."

* * *

The first false wall closed behind them before they had gone twenty steps, and the passage ahead changed shape. Rowan put his spear through a seam and pulled, and the wall did not move.

"It is a false wall," Lysette said.

"I know it is a false wall. I am trying to open it."

"Try the other side." Felix was crouched by the floor with one hand flat on the stone. Trail came to him as a line of light in the air, thin and pale, showing where boots had passed and where stone had been dragged back and forth. It ran under the wall and out again three paces to the east. "It slides. Not opens. The tracks turn there."

The line faded where the stone had been dragged and came back three paces east, then stopped at a place where the floor was clean. "Someone sweeps the tracks," Felix said. "Every night."

"Why?" Rowan asked.

"So runners cannot read them." Felix looked at the wall. "Except us."

"How do you see that?" Rowan asked.

"Do not ask," Felix said. "Just go where the line goes."

The false wall had a number cut into the stone on the east side and a date from three years ago. Felix read it aloud. "They ran this wall in the autumn."

"It still works," Lysette said. "That is the trouble with walls."

A whistle went once from the gallery. "Two minutes," Okoru called. "You are talking more than you are moving." Lysette shut her book and led them east.

She put a hand on the wall and closed her eyes. Witness came as ghost-light in the air behind her, faint as moonlight on water: a figure in a dark coat pushing the wall from the far side, then the wall sliding into place. The replay showed where the hand had been and how far the stone had travelled. It needed a still place and a quiet moment, and while it lasted she could not move.

Eden stood at her back for those few breaths without being asked. He faced the dark passage with his shield low and counted what might come out of it: one breath, two, six. Nothing came. He noticed that he was standing exactly where he would have stood on a Vigil, with his weight on his front foot. He shifted it back until he looked like a boy who was only waiting. Lysette opened her eyes, glanced once at where he stood, and wrote something short in her book.

"Two hands," she said. "One at the top, one at the bottom. Push from the east."

Rowan and Eden pushed together, and the wall slid aside with a grind that made Clara wince. Beyond it the passage turned left and then left again.

A construct came at them from the turn, moving at Year 5 speed, with a wooden blade and a red tag at its wrist. The tag said THIRTY-FIVE PER CENT, and this one had no cut wire. The brass at its elbow had been replaced recently and shone under the lamp. Rowan stepped into its path and took the first swing on his spear. Clara moved to his left where the blade could not reach without turning its back.

Rowan's spear hit the brass at its elbow. The construct swung, missed, and came again. Clara's hands went to Rowan's forearm for one breath. The red mark on his knuckles closed from the edges in. She looked pale after it and said nothing.

Eden blocked what came past Rowan. He did not strike. He let the blade slide off his shield and made sure it went into the wall rather than back toward the others. The construct came again with the blade high. Rowan stepped inside it, and his spear caught the brass at the shoulder. The thing turned on its own foot and tried to bring the blade across his back. Eden put his shield into the space behind Rowan's knee. The blade rang on it and went wide.

"I had it," Rowan said.

"I know," Eden said. "You had it with me."

A mediocre block, at the right angle, was the whole job.

Felix's line showed the second construct before they saw it. "Left passage," he said. "It has been standing there for four minutes. There is a fresh track going out."

"Fresh track?" Lysette asked.

"Someone walked past it recently and did not come back." Felix stood up. "That means it is waiting for us."

Lysette counted the lamps under her breath. "Then we go now, before it waits better."

They went. The construct came out of the side passage with its blade low. Rowan took it on the spear again while Lysette went in with her two batons, left tilted higher. She did not try to break it. She put a stick under its wrist and turned it so the blade point went into the floor, and Rowan's spear came down on the brass joint. The construct stopped.

Lysette kept both batons up until Okoru's whistle went. Then she put them away and flexed her wrist. "That is two."

"Two what?" Rowan asked.

"Two things we did together."

Clara put a hand on Rowan's sleeve. "You took a knock."

"It is nothing."

"It was a knock." She let go, and when Rowan had moved on she sat down for two steps before she stood again. "Keep going."

Eden saw her do it. He saw Lysette see it too, and saw Lysette decide, with a small tightening of her jaw, to say nothing in front of Rowan. It was the second time in a week that the two of them had hidden the same thing from the same boy. Neither of them looked at the other.

"You are pale," Lysette said.

"I am always pale."

"No. You are paler."

The passage ended at a wall with no door and no seam. Felix's line stopped there. Rowan pushed, and Lysette pushed, and nothing moved.

Eden looked at the lamps. The last lamp's shadow lay across the wall, except for a thin edge of dark that ran up the stone and vanished at the ceiling. There was a vent above the wall. Moonlight came through it and fell on the floor behind the wall, so the wall threw no shadow of its own in that one place.

"The wall is shorter than it looks," he said.

Lysette turned. "Where?"

"Above the lamp. The shadow stops there. There is a gap."

"You found that a minute ago."

"I found it when we came in."

She looked at him for half a second, then turned to the others. "Rowan, lift me. Felix, check the line beyond. We go up over the wall, not through it."

Felix went to the floor and put his hand flat. "The line turns left on the other side. There is a passage."

Rowan crouched and made a step of his hands, and Lysette climbed up without losing her book. Clara went next, then Felix, then Rowan, and Eden last. On the other side the passage was narrow and wet. It ran left for fifteen steps to a door with a bell above it.

Eden rang the bell, and the stopwatch outside clicked.

"Eleven minutes," Okoru said from the gallery above them. "Order: Felix, Lysette, Wren, Clara, Rowan. Third."

Okoru wrote something in his own book and turned the board around so every Lance could see. The first Lance, the fourth, ours. In that order. The other five names sat in a column underneath with no marks beside them. Lysette copied the order into her book without being asked.

Eden's hand had been on the bell rope since they came through the door. His fingers were stiff when he opened them.

"Third," Felix said. "Do we get a ribbon for third?"

"You get a mark," Okoru said. "It is better than no mark."

"I will take it."

Rowan stared at the board. "Third? Lance One did nine." He read the column twice with his lips moving, and his face did the thing it did when he was trying to be a good loser and was not yet sure how. "I was loud," he said. "Was I loud?"

"You were loud," Lysette said.

"Was it bad?"

"It was useful. Twice."

Rowan took that as a prize and held it for the rest of the afternoon.

"Lance One did nine," Okoru said. "You did eleven and you brought everyone through. That is third and it is not nothing."

Felix sat down on the step and put his book on his knees. "I found a line."

"A line," Rowan said, "is not a door."

"It was a line to a door." Felix held up one finger. "I am counting that as a door."

From the gallery, a man in a staff coat watched Rowan. He had a narrow face and a pen in one hand, and he stood where the lamps did not reach. He wrote something on a paper on his wrist. He did not look at the door or at the stopwatch. He looked at Rowan's hands on the spear, and then at Rowan's face when Clara spoke to him. The pen moved twice. Then the gallery lamp went out and the staff coat was only a shape against the dark. Eden looked up once, and the space under the lamp was empty.

"Did you see that?" he asked.

"See what?" Lysette said, with her pencil ready.

"Nothing." Eden took his hand off the bell. "Third is enough."

Felix kept looking at the board while Rowan told Clara that the construct had tried to hit him from behind. "It did not," Clara said.

"It turned."

"It turned toward my spear."

"That is the same as behind." Felix took one finger away from the count. "I am counting that as a door as well."
