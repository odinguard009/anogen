# Chapter 11: The Graded Maze

The maze was in the basement. Stone walls. No ceiling. Lamps on the walls. The floor was stone. It was cold.

Twenty students stood at the entrance. Okoru stood beside them. "The maze changes," he said. "Walls move. Paths close. You have ten minutes. Find the center. Get out. If you are still inside when the walls close, you fail."

He pointed at the entrance. "Go."

The students went in. Eden went last. He watched the others enter. He counted. Three seconds between each. The walls did not move yet.

He stepped in. The maze was dark. The lamps were dim. He walked slowly. He touched the wall with his left hand. Always left. That was the rule. Keep your hand on one wall and follow it. You will find the center or the exit.

The wall was cold. Stone. He walked. Left hand on the wall. Right hand holding his lance.

After thirty steps, the wall moved. It shifted left. Eden stopped. He waited. The wall stopped. He kept going.

He heard voices. Other students. Lost. Calling out. He did not call back. Calling back meant being found. Being found meant being followed.

He reached a junction. Two paths. Left and right. He chose left. His hand stayed on the wall.

The path narrowed. Then it opened into a small room. In the center of the room was a stone. On the stone was a mark. A circle with a line through it. That was the center.

Eden touched the mark. It was cold. He turned. The way back was different. The walls had moved. He could not go back the same way.

He followed the wall again. Left hand. Always left. The path twisted. It went up. It went down. He climbed a short stair. He went through a narrow gap.

He heard a sound. Footsteps. Behind him. Fast. He turned. A student ran past. The student did not see him. The student was panicking.

Eden kept walking. Left hand on the wall. The wall moved again. This time it moved right. Eden had to let go. He stood in the middle of a corridor. No wall to follow.

He closed his eyes. He listened. He heard water. Dripping. To his left. He walked toward the sound. The corridor ended at a door. The door was open. Outside was the exit.

He stepped out. The light was bright. Okoru stood there. He looked at his watch.

"Seven minutes," Okoru said. "You are the first."

Eden looked back. Other students came out one by one. Lysette came out at eight minutes. She was breathing hard. Her hair was messy. The braid had come loose.

"Good," Okoru said. "You all passed. Barely."

He looked at Eden. "You did not panic. The others panicked. You kept your hand on the wall. That is the lesson. When you are lost, find something solid. Hold it. Follow it."

Eden nodded. He thought about the maze. The walls moved. But the wall he held did not change. It was always there. He had followed it.

Lysette stood beside him. "How did you find the center?" she asked.

"I followed the wall," Eden said.

"That is too simple."

"Simple works," Eden said.

Lysette looked at him. She did not argue. She knew he was right.

They left the basement. The sun was bright. The academy was normal. Eden thought about the maze. It was like life. Walls moved. Paths closed. But if you held something solid, you found your way.

He held his notebook. He wrote: "Maze. Left hand on wall. Find solid. Follow."

He closed the notebook. He walked to class. He was on time.
