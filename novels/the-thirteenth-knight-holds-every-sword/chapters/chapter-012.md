# Chapter 12: Perrin

Perrin was small. He had brown hair and brown eyes. He was not strong. He was not fast. He was in the bottom ten of the class. He sat in the back row. He did not speak unless spoken to. He ate alone. He walked alone. He was alone.

Eden sat with him at lunch. Perrin ate slowly. He looked at his food like it might bite him.

"You are quiet," Perrin said.

"I am eating," Eden said.

"You are always quiet. Even when you eat."

Eden ate. He did not answer. Perrin looked at his own food. He pushed a piece of bread around the plate.

"Can I ask you something?" Perrin said.

"Yes."

"How do you do it? The fighting. The construct. The maze. You make it look easy."

"It is not easy," Eden said.

"Then how?"

Eden put down his bread. He looked at Perrin. Perrin's eyes were brown. They were scared. "I count. I watch. I wait. When I see a chance, I take it. That is all."

Perrin looked at him. "I cannot count. I panic. When the construct swung, I froze. I could not move."

"That is normal," Eden said.

"Is it? The others did not freeze."

"The others are different from you," Eden said. "You are you. You freeze. So you need a plan for freezing."

Perrin looked confused. "A plan for freezing?"

"When you freeze, you stop. So you need to stop on purpose. Choose when to stop. Then you are not panicking. You are pausing. Pausing is not freezing. Pausing is thinking."

Perrin thought about this. "So if I freeze on purpose, I am thinking?"

"Yes," Eden said. "Then you count. One, two, three. Then you move."

Perrin nodded slowly. "One, two, three. Then move."

"Yes."

Perrin ate his bread. He looked less scared. Eden ate his own bread. It was stale. He did not mind.

They sat in silence for a while. The dining hall was loud. Students talked. Laughed. Argued. Eden and Perrin did not talk. They ate.

After lunch, they went to class. Perrin walked beside Eden. He was still nervous. But his steps were steadier.

In class, Okoru taught a new move. The hinge. A pivot on the lead foot. The lance swings around like a door.

Eden tried it. It felt natural. Like opening a door.

Perrin tried it. He stumbled. His feet were wrong. Okoru corrected him. Perrin tried again. Better.

After class, Perrin came to Eden. "One, two, three," he said. "Then move."

Eden nodded. "Good."

Perrin smiled. It was small. But it was real.

Eden watched him go. Perrin was not strong. He was not fast. But he was trying. That was enough.

Eden went to the roof garden. He sat on the bench. He wrote in his notebook: "Perrin. Freezes. Plan: pause, count, move. He is trying."

He closed the notebook. The wind moved the plants. The city hummed.

He thought about Perrin. Not everyone was strong. Not everyone was fast. But everyone could learn. If they had a plan.

Eden had a plan. He always had a plan.

---

The next day, Eden went to the arms yard. He trained alone. The yard was empty. The sun was up. The stone walls were grey.

He practiced the hinge. Pivot. Swing. The lance moved like a door. He did it ten times. Twenty times. Fifty times.

His arms were tired. His hands were sore. He did not stop.

He thought about Perrin. Perrin would practice too. Perrin would stumble. Perrin would try again. Perrin would get better.

Eden practiced the Latch. The shield catches a blade and locks it against the knocker ring. He did it ten times. Twenty times.

He thought about the maze. The walls moved. But the wall he held did not change. It was always there.

He practiced the Draught. The lance through the narrow gap in a guard, like wind through a door. He did it ten times.

He was sweating. His shirt was wet. He did not stop.

He thought about the Veil. The Veil that spoke. The Veil that waited. The Veil that was not normal.

He stopped. He put down his lance. He sat on the bench. He breathed.

He thought about the clerk. The clerk who was scared. The clerk who had a family. The clerk who let them pass.

He thought about the teacher. The teacher who reported him. The teacher who smiled. The teacher who was with the Ashen Crowns.

He thought about the warrant. Friday. The hearing. Hildegard. The registrar.

He had a lot to do. He always had a lot to do.

He picked up his lance. He practiced one more time. Then he stopped. He went to the dining hall.

He ate lunch. He sat alone. He did not taste the food. He thought about Perrin. He thought about the maze. He thought about the Veil.

After lunch, he went to the library. He looked for books about the Ashen Crowns. He found one. It was old. The cover was red.

He opened it. The pages were yellow. The writing was small. He read.

"The Ashen Crowns are a terror network. They are the heirs of the eleven kingdoms conquered by the Iron King. They seek revenge. They use Veils. They use bombs. They use fear."

Eden read this. He knew about the Ashen Crowns. But the book gave him details. Dates. Names. Places.

He wrote in his notebook: "Ashen Crowns. Heirs of conquered kingdoms. Revenge. Use Veils, bombs, fear. Read more."

He closed the book. He put it back. He would come back tomorrow.

He went to the roof garden. He sat on the bench. He looked at the city. The lamps glowed. The trams ran. The city was normal.

But the city was not safe. The Veils were in the walls. The lamps were their eyes. The clerk was watching. The teacher was watching. The Ashen Crowns were watching.

Eden watched back. He always watched back.

He closed his notebook. He lay back on the bench. He looked at the sky. The clouds moved. The wind blew.

He thought about Perrin. Perrin was trying. Perrin was learning. Perrin would be okay.

He thought about Lysette. Lysette was watching the teacher. Lysette was careful. Lysette would be okay.

He thought about Felix. Felix was loyal. Felix was strong. Felix would be okay.

He thought about himself. He was not okay. He was not fine. But he did not know how to be not fine. He did not know how to say it. He did not know how to feel it.

He knew facts. He knew plans. He knew numbers. He did not know feelings.

He closed his eyes. He did not sleep. He thought. He always thought.

The wind moved the plants. The city hummed. The sun went down.

Eden sat on the roof. He watched the city. He planned. He always planned.
