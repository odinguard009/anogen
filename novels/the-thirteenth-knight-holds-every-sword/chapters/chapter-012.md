# Chapter 12: Perrin

Perrin was small. He had brown hair and brown eyes. He was not strong. He was not fast. He was in the bottom ten of the class.

Eden sat with him at lunch. Perrin ate slowly. He looked at his food like it might bite him.

"You are quiet," Perrin said.

"I am eating," Eden said.

"You are always quiet. Even when you eat."

Eden ate. He did not answer. Perrin looked at his own food. He pushed a piece of bread around the plate.

"Can I ask you something?" Perrin said.

"Yes."

"How do you do it? The fighting. The construct. The maze. You make it look easy."

"It is not easy," Eden said.

"Then how?"

Eden put down his bread. "I count. I watch. I wait. When I see a chance, I take it. That is all."

Perrin looked at him. "I cannot count. I panic. When the construct swung, I froze. I could not move."

"That is normal," Eden said.

"Is it? The others did not freeze."

"The others are different from you," Eden said. "You are you. You freeze. So you need a plan for freezing."

Perrin looked confused. "A plan for freezing?"

"When you freeze, you stop. So you need to stop on purpose. Choose when to stop. Then you are not panicking. You are pausing. Pausing is not freezing. Pausing is thinking."

Perrin thought about this. "So if I freeze on purpose, I am thinking?"

"Yes," Eden said. "Then you count. One, two, three. Then you move."

Perrin nodded slowly. "One, two, three. Then move."

"Yes."

Perrin ate his bread. He looked less scared. Eden ate his own bread. It was stale. He did not mind.

After lunch, they went to class. Perrin walked beside Eden. He was still nervous. But his steps were steadier.

In class, Okoru taught a new move. The hinge. A pivot on the lead foot. The lance swings around like a door.

Eden tried it. It felt natural. Like opening a door.

Perrin tried it. He stumbled. His feet were wrong. Okoru corrected him. Perrin tried again. Better.

After class, Perrin came to Eden. "One, two, three," he said. "Then move."

Eden nodded. "Good."

Perrin smiled. It was small. But it was real.

Eden watched him go. Perrin was not strong. He was not fast. But he was trying. That was enough.

Eden went to the roof garden. He sat on the bench. He wrote in his notebook: "Perrin. Freezes. Plan: pause, count, move. He is trying."

He closed the notebook. The wind moved the plants. The city hummed.

He thought about Perrin. Not everyone was strong. Not everyone was fast. But everyone could learn. If they had a plan.

Eden had a plan. He always had a plan.
