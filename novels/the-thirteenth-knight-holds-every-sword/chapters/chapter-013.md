# Chapter 13: The Lamp House

The lamp house was at the edge of the city. It was small. One floor. Stone walls. A door. No windows. It stood alone on a hill. The hill was bare. No trees. No grass. Just dirt and stone.

Eden went at night. He wore his black armor under his coat. His lance was short. **[Inead]** was on his back.

Lysette was with him. She had insisted. She carried a lantern. The lantern light was yellow and weak.

"What are we looking for?" she asked.

"Evidence," Eden said.

"Of what?"

"The Veil in the exchange. She controlled wires. The lamp house controls the city lamps. If she could control wires, she could control lamps."

Lysette looked at the lamp house. "You think there is another Veil here."

"I think there might be."

They walked up the hill. The door was locked. Eden touched it. The lock was old. He took a small tool from his pocket. He opened the lock in ten seconds.

Inside, it was dark. Lysette raised her lantern. The room was small. Machines lined the walls. Wires everywhere. Switches. Dials. The air smelled like oil and hot metal.

Eden looked at the machines. He touched a wire. It was warm. The machines were running. But the lamps outside were on. Someone was controlling them.

He looked at the floor. There were footprints. Fresh. In the dust. Two sets. One large. One small.

"Two people," Eden said.

"Or two Veils," Lysette said.

Eden followed the footprints. They went to the back of the room. There was a door. He opened it. Behind it was a small room. A bed. A table. A chair. On the table was a notebook.

Eden picked up the notebook. He opened it. The pages were filled with writing. Not English. Not any language he knew. Symbols. Circles. Lines. Like a code.

"What is it?" Lysette asked.

"I do not know," Eden said. "But it is not human."

He put the notebook in his coat. He looked around the room. Under the bed was a box. He pulled it out. Inside were papers. Letters. They were in English. He read one.

It said: "The exchange is secure. The clerk is ours. The lamps will be ready by Friday. Do not fail."

Eden read it again. "The lamps will be ready by Friday." Friday. The day of his hearing. The day he had to attend class.

"They are planning something for Friday," Eden said.

Lysette read the letter. Her face went pale. "The lamps. If they control the lamps, they can turn them off. The whole city."

"Yes," Eden said.

"We have to tell someone."

"Not yet," Eden said. "We need more. We need to know who 'ours' is. We need to know who sent this."

Lysette looked at him. "Eden. If they turn off the lamps, people will die. Veils will move in the dark."

"I know," Eden said. "But if we tell now, they will know we found this. They will run. We need to catch them."

Lysette was quiet. Then she said, "How long?"

"Three days," Eden said. "Friday. We watch until then."

They left the lamp house. Eden locked the door. They walked down the hill. The city lamps glowed. They looked normal. But Eden knew they were not.

He walked home. He did not sleep. He sat on his bed and read the notebook. The symbols meant nothing to him. But he would find out.

He always found out.

---

The next day, Eden went to the roof garden. He sat on the bench. He looked at the lamp house on the hill. It was dark. No light. But the machines were running. He could see the glow from the windows. Faint. Blue.

He watched. An hour passed. Nothing happened. Two hours. Nothing.

At 2 AM, a light appeared at the lamp house. A door opened. A figure came out. Tall. Dark coat. The figure walked down the hill. Toward the city.

Eden followed. He stayed on the roofs. He jumped from building to building. The city was quiet. Only the trams were still. They did not run at night.

The figure walked to the exchange. The telephone exchange. The same one where Eden had killed the Veil. The figure went inside.

Eden waited on the roof across the street. He watched the windows. Lights went on and off. The figure was doing something inside.

After twenty minutes, the figure came out. It walked back up the hill. To the lamp house.

Eden followed again. He watched from a rooftop. The figure entered the lamp house. The door closed. The lights went out.

Eden sat on the roof. He thought. The figure went to the exchange. Then came back. It was checking something. Or it was reporting.

He needed to know who the figure was. He needed a face.

The next night, he went again. Same time. Same place. The figure came out. Eden was ready. He had a small mirror. He used it to catch the light from a street lamp. He flashed it toward the figure's face.

The figure stopped. It turned. It looked up. For one second, Eden saw the face.

It was the clerk from the exchange. The same clerk who had let them pass. The same clerk who had smiled.

Eden put down the mirror. The clerk stood in the street. Looking up. Looking at the roof where Eden was. Then the clerk turned and walked back up the hill.

Eden left. He went home. He sat on his bed. He wrote in his notebook: "Clerk. Exchange. Lamp house. Same person. He is the one."

He closed the notebook. He lay back. He thought about the clerk. A clerk who controlled wires. A clerk who controlled lamps. A clerk who was not a Veil but worked with Veils.

Eden did not sleep. He planned. Friday was three days away. He had three days to plan.

He would need help. He did not want help. But he would need it.

He thought about Lysette. She would want to help. He thought about Vesk. Vesk could watch. He thought about Felix. Felix was loyal.

He would use them. All of them. But he would not tell them everything. He would tell them what they needed to know.

He closed his eyes. He did not sleep. He planned.
