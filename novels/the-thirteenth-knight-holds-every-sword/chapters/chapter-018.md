# Chapter 18: The Quiet Tail

Eden followed a man. Not the clerk. A different man. The man was tall. He wore a dark coat. He walked slowly. He looked at the buildings as he walked. He was not lost. He was checking.

Eden stayed fifty steps behind. He did not hide. He walked like a student. Hands in pockets. Head down. No one looked at a student.

The man walked to the exchange. He went inside. He came out five minutes later. He walked to the Records Office. He went inside. He came out ten minutes later. He walked to the lamp house on the hill.

Eden followed. He watched from the bottom of the hill. The man went inside. He came out twenty minutes later. He walked back to the city.

Eden did not follow him back. He went to the lamp house. He unlocked the door. He went in. The machines were running. The switch was untouched. But there were new papers on the table.

Eden read them. They were lists. Names. Addresses. Times. The lists were for the city lamps. Each lamp house had a time. A time when it would be turned off.

Lamp House 7: 8 PM. Friday. The same as before.

But there were other lamp houses. Lamp House 3: 8 PM. Friday. Lamp House 9: 8 PM. Friday. Three lamp houses. Three switches. Three parts of the city would go dark.

Eden took the papers. He put them in his coat. He locked the door. He went home.

He sat on his bed. He read the papers again. Three lamp houses. Three switches. He could not be in three places at once.

He needed help. He did not want help. But he needed it.

He went to Vesk. Vesk was on his usual roof. Eating an apple.

"I need more," Eden said.

"You always need more," Vesk said.

"The Ashen Crowns. Who is in charge? Here. In the city."

Vesk looked at him. His bright eyes narrowed. "You do not want to know."

"I do."

Vesk was quiet. He ate his apple. He looked at the city. Then he said, "A man named Mott. He runs the cell here. He is in the exchange. Second floor. Behind a door that does not open."

"How do you know?"

"I watch. I listen. I see."

"Can you get me in?"

Vesk smiled. "For a price."

Eden gave him a coin. Vesk took it. "Tomorrow night. I will leave the back door open. One minute. That is all you get."

"Thank you," Eden said.

Vesk shrugged. "Do not thank me. I am a tool."

"I know," Eden said. "Tools are useful."

Vesk finished his apple. Eden left.

He went home. He sat on his bed. He planned. Tomorrow night. The exchange. Mott. One minute.

He would need to be fast. He would need to be quiet. He would need to see everything in one minute.

He could do it. He always did.

He slept. No dreams.
