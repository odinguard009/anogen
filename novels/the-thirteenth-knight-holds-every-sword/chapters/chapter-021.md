# Chapter 21: The Locket

Lysette found a locket. It was in a drawer in the Discipline Committee office. It was small. Gold. It had a picture inside. A boy. Young. Smiling.

Lysette looked at the picture. She knew the boy. It was Corentin. Her brother. He had died two years ago.

She had not seen this locket before. It had been hidden. Someone had put it in the drawer. Recently.

She picked it up. She held it. It was cold. She opened it again. The boy smiled at her. She remembered that smile. She remembered his laugh. She remembered his voice.

She closed the locket. She put it in her pocket. She went to the roof garden. Eden was there.

She sat beside him. She did not speak. She held the locket.

Eden looked at her. He saw the locket. He did not ask. He waited.

Lysette opened the locket. She showed Eden. "My brother," she said.

Eden looked at the picture. "Corentin," he said.

"You know his name?"

"I know many names," Eden said.

Lysette closed the locket. "He died two years ago. They said it was an accident. I do not believe it."

"What do you believe?" Eden asked.

"I believe someone killed him. Someone who did not want him to talk."

"Talk about what?"

"I do not know. He was investigating something. Before he died. He was always investigating. He was like me."

Eden was quiet. He thought about Corentin. He had heard the name. In a file. In the Thirteenth Vigil records. Corentin Hollen. Killed by a Veil. Eden had killed the Veil. But he had not told Lysette.

He could not tell her. Not now. Not like this.

"Lysette," Eden said. "I am sorry about your brother."

Lysette looked at him. Her eyes were wet. "Do not be sorry. Find out who did it."

"I will," Eden said.

He would find out. He already knew. But he would find out how to tell her. When. And how much.

Lysette put the locket away. She wiped her eyes. "I am fine," she said.

"You are not fine," Eden said.

"I know. But I will be."

She left. Eden sat on the bench. He looked at the city. He thought about Corentin. He thought about the Veil he had killed. He thought about Lysette.

He had killed the thing that killed her brother. She did not know. She might never know. Or she might find out. And then she would hate him. Or she would thank him. Or both.

Eden did not know which was worse.

He sat on the bench. The wind moved the plants. The city hummed.

He did not sleep that night. He sat on the roof. He watched the city. He thought about Corentin. He thought about Lysette. He thought about the truth.

The truth was heavy. He carried it. He always carried it.
