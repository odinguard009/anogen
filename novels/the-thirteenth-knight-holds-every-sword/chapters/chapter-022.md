# Chapter 22: The Dye-Works

The dye-works was near the river. It was a large building. Stone walls. Tall chimney. Smoke. The air smelled like chemicals and hot water.

Eden went at night. He wore dark clothes. No armor. He did not want to be seen.

The dye-works made cloth. Blue cloth. Red cloth. Green cloth. The cloth was for the army. Uniforms. Banners.

Eden had a reason to be here. The lamp house papers had listed the dye-works. Not as a lamp house. As a contact. Someone at the dye-works was working with the Ashen Crowns.

He went in through a side door. It was unlocked. Inside, the air was hot and wet. Vats of dye. Blue. Red. Green. The colors were bright. They glowed in the lamplight.

He walked between the vats. His feet were quiet. The floor was wet. He stepped on the dry parts.

He heard voices. Two men. Talking. He stopped. He listened.

"...the lamps. Friday. All three."

"I know. I will be there."

"And the girl?"

"What girl?"

"The one with the notebook. The Discipline Committee."

"She is not a problem."

"She might be. She is asking questions."

Eden recognized one voice. It was the teacher. The one who had reported him. The one who had smiled.

Eden stayed still. He listened.

"I will handle the girl," the teacher said. "She trusts me."

"You had better," the other man said. "The Ashen Crowns do not forgive failure."

Eden stepped back. His foot splashed in a puddle. The sound was small. But the men heard it.

"Who is there?" the teacher called.

Eden did not answer. He moved. Fast. Through the vats. Behind a wall. The men came. They had lamps. The light moved through the vats.

Eden stayed still. He breathed slowly. In. Out. In. Out.

The men searched. They did not find him. They went back to talking.

"We should go," the other man said. "It is late."

"Yes," the teacher said.

They left. Eden waited. He counted to one hundred. Then he left.

He went home. He sat on his bed. He wrote in his notebook: "Teacher. Dye-works. Ashen Crowns. He will handle Lysette. Friday."

He closed the notebook. He thought about Lysette. The teacher had said, "She trusts me." Lysette did trust him. The teacher had smiled at her. He had said she was a good student.

Eden had to warn her. But he could not tell her everything. He could not tell her about Corentin. Not yet.

He would tell her about the teacher. That was enough. For now.

He went to the roof garden. Lysette was there. She was reading. She looked up.

"Eden. What is wrong?"

"The teacher," Eden said. "The one who reported me. He is with the Ashen Crowns. He plans to handle you."

Lysette's face went still. "Handle me?"

"He said you trust him. He will use that."

Lysette looked at her notebook. "I trusted him. He smiled at me."

"People smile," Eden said. "It does not mean they are kind."

Lysette was quiet. Then she said, "What do we do?"

"We continue the plan. Friday. But you do not go near him. You stay with me."

"I can take care of myself," Lysette said.

"I know. But this is different. He is not a Veil. He is a person. A person who smiles and lies."

Lysette nodded. She did not argue. She knew Eden was right.

They sat in silence. The wind moved the plants. The city hummed.

Eden thought about the teacher. He would have to deal with him. Not now. Later. After Friday. After the lamps.

He would deal with him. He always dealt with things.
