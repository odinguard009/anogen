# Chapter 25: The Lattice

"Go and get the guard," Eden said.

"Which guard?"

"The one at the west door. He has a grey coat and a short stick."

"You have seen him?"

"I watched him come in." Eden kept his hand on the clouded glass. "He has not moved since the eleventh bell."

Lysette did not move. Below them the Ember with the blade at the prefect's throat had begun to count the hostages aloud, and every count made the room quieter.

"If I go, they will see me," she said.

"They will see a girl going for help. That is what they expect."

"Then you will be alone."

"Yes."

She looked at him for one second, and then she was gone down the stair with her gown held up in one hand, fast enough that the door did not swing shut behind her. Eden counted ten steps before he went to the glass.

The roof of the atrium was a lattice of iron bars with panes of glass between them. The broken lanterns had left only the moon and two work lamps, and the lamps threw the lattice's shadow down across the dance floor in a grid of dark squares with clean edges. Eden needed those edges. He opened a Door in the nearest square and stepped out of the roof and into the hall beside the north table, where no one was looking because everyone was watching the man with the blade.

The first thing he noted was the number: forty students and staff standing still in two clumps, with an Ember at each clump's edge and a knife at a throat in every third pair. The second thing was that the skilled ones could not move either. Rowan stood with his spear point on the floor and Clara's hand on his sleeve. Seraphine held Felix's wrist with two fingers, and Felix had stopped looking at her card and was looking at the knife by her neck. Valerius stood at the west side with his hands open and his jaw set, and a man behind him had a blade laid against the bronze pin on his collar.

A woman in a pale coat stood in the middle of the floor with her right sleeve pinned up at the shoulder. Frost-white lines ran from her collar down her arm under the skin, and her right hand was grey at the fingertips. She held the hand out as she spoke, and the room listened because the crown on her lapel had a name on it that everybody knew.

"Ilsabet Rorke," she said, to no one in particular. "Crown of Kaldmark. That is the old title. You may call me what you like."

"Call her nothing," the Ember at the prefect's throat said. "She gives the orders."

"I give the orders," Ilsabet said, "and you keep the knife where it is. Is that clear?"

"Yes, my lady."

"Then stop counting. It makes me tired."

She was the one who had given the order about two guests. Eden had come through the Door three paces behind her left shoulder, and he waited while she finished, because a hostage with a knife at her throat was not a target he could take yet.

"Where is the boy?" she asked. The Ember under the north table pointed up. She looked up through the broken lanterns and smiled at the glass. "Then we have him already."

Eden knocked twice on the air beside the lattice shadow that lay across her coat.

The Door opened at her feet and her right foot went into it up to the ankle. She did not fall; she had been a duellist before the grey took her hand. Her **[Hrak]** came up out of the sleeve like a club, the grey swelling from shoulder to fist, and she brought it round at the empty place where Eden stood. The air in front of him went cold and heavy, and the grey in her arm spread another inch up her neck with the effort. Eden went under the swing and came up behind her, and her hand closed on nothing but the shadow of a lattice bar.

He needed one cut, and she was too well guarded from the front. He took a blow on the shield first.

An Ember at the west clump threw a short blade at the sound of the Door. Eden caught it on **[Exa]**'s folded face, flat overhead, with the iron ring toward the ceiling. The blade struck the ring and rang, and the sound went through the hall like a bell someone had hit by mistake, and every hostage looked up.

Then Eden drew **[Inead]** under the shield in one motion.

**[Lintel]** was the shield held flat and the gold sword coming out from under it: a door of black plate above his head and a cut that started where the shield ended. Gold light filled the hall for one breath and made the grey in Ilsabet's arm honest. The cut went through the frost lines at her shoulder and stopped before her neck. She dropped to her knees with her grey hand still open, and the crown on her lapel came loose and slid across the floor.

The hostages did not see a man in black armor. They saw a dark shape where the light was wrong and a gold glint that came and went in one motion. A girl by the north table later said the lantern must have fallen again. Felix looked at the empty space above her and said, "It did fall. I heard it." "No, you did not," Seraphine said. "You were looking at your book." "I was looking at the card." "The card is on the floor." Felix looked down and put his foot on it, hard. "Then I heard it." Valerius saw more than the others because he had been looking straight at the north table. He saw a shield where no shield had been and a sword that shone like a low sun. He did not move or speak. The man behind him with the blade did not know that his prince had seen anything at all. Valerius's hands stayed open at his sides, and his thumb pressed once against his palm, and then he was looking at Ilsabet on the floor as if he had been looking there all along.

The Embers stepped back when Eden walked across the broken glass toward them. They did not know why. Two of them had their blades at throats and did not lower them, but their feet moved, and that was enough. Seraphine's hand, which had been shaking since the chandelier, went still again, and she said something to Felix under her breath that nobody heard.

Eden did not kill Ilsabet. She was down with her grey hand open and her crown on the floor, and a kill without proof was not a thing he did. He put a cut through the brass at her wrist instead, where the grey lines ended, and the hand went slack on the stone. She looked up at the dark shape over her with a face that had lost its certainty.

"Take them outside," she said, to her own people. "All of them. Now."

A woman among the hostages spoke from the west clump, in a dry, interested voice, without moving her hands: "That wall hanging is Ostrevaal work, you know. The blue border was added later." It was Merrow, in a council coat with a speck of cake on his cuff, and he was looking at the wall behind the Crowns rather than at the blades. Nobody answered him. A student near him said, "Sir, there is a knife at your collar," and Merrow put two fingers up to the blade as if he were checking a button.

"So there is," he said. "Do not let it worry you. It has been there ten minutes and has done nothing." He turned his head a little for a better view of the wall. "The blue border is later, though. The rest is original."

"My lady," the Ember said to Ilsabet, "the council man is talking."

"Let him," she said. "He is the only one who is not frightened."

The Embers pulled back toward the east gallery with the hostages in front of them, and Eden followed through the lattice shadows. Each dark square on the floor had an edge where a work lamp touched it, and each edge was enough. He opened one Door under a prefect, closed it behind her, opened another under Rowan and Clara, and a third under a table where two girls had been sitting face down. The Crowns could not tell which of their prisoners had moved, because the prisoners themselves were not sure. Felix went through one of the patches with his hand out and came out on the other side of his own shadow, and he said, "I like this less every time," to nobody in particular. Felix's hand came free of Seraphine's wrist in one of the gaps. Rowan's spear went down and came up in Clara's hand instead, and Clara did not look at him while she did it.

At the east door the last Ember turned with his blade at a prefect's neck and found nothing to cut. The hall behind him was full of smoke from the work lamps and broken lanterns, and the dark shapes in it had stopped being separate from the shadows.

Lysette was standing at the west door with a guard in a grey coat beside her and her black book shut under her arm. She had seen smoke where there should have been a man and too little of anything else, and she did not look at Eden when he came down the last step of the roof stair.

"Is everyone out?" she asked the guard, not Eden.

"The hall is empty but for us, miss."

"Good."

"The guard will take it from here," she said to Eden. "I have written what I saw. It is not much."

"No."

"It is never much with you." She opened the book and put one line on the page, then shut it again. "I saw smoke in a shape that should have been a man, and a light under it, and then the light was gone. I will not write the rest because there is no word for it." She looked past him at the roof stair. "Go back up before they count heads. I will say you were with me the whole time."

"That is not true."

"It is the form I am using."

"And if the guard asks?"

"He will not." She put the pencil in the book. "He is a good guard. He knows when a form has been written."
