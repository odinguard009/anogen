# Chapter 26: The Roof Dance

The roof garden. Midnight. Eden sat on the bench. The city was dark. The lamps glowed.

Lysette came. She climbed the stairs to the roof. She stood at the door. She looked at Eden.

"You are here," she said.

"I am always here," Eden said.

Lysette walked to the bench. She sat beside him. She had her notebook. She did not open it.

"I have not slept," she said.

"Neither have I," Eden said.

They sat in silence. The wind moved the plants. The city hummed. A tram passed below. Its bell rang. Distant.

"Eden," Lysette said.

"Yes."

"Thank you. For the Ball. For dancing with me."

"You taught me," Eden said.

"I know. But you did not have to."

Eden looked at her. Her face was serious. Her blue-violet eyes were steady. The strand of hair fell over her forehead.

"You are my friend," Eden said.

Lysette was quiet. Then she said, "Is that all?"

Eden did not answer. He looked at the city. The lamps. The motor-cars. The trams.

"I do not know what you are," Lysette said. "You are quiet. You are strange. You disappear at night. You fight like a soldier. You fix clocks. You sit on the roof."

She looked at him. "But you are my friend. And I am glad."

Eden felt something. Not in his mind. In his chest. Warm. Small. He did not name it. He did not know its name.

"Thank you," he said.

Lysette stood. "Good night, Eden."

"Good night, Lysette."

She left. Eden sat on the bench. He looked at the city. The warmth in his chest did not go away. It stayed.

He did not sleep. He sat on the roof. He watched the lamps. He thought about Lysette. He thought about the warmth.

He did not name it. He did not need to.

---

The next morning, Eden went to the arms yard. He trained alone. The yard was empty. The sun was up. The stone walls were grey.

He practiced the hinge. Pivot. Swing. The lance moved like a door. He did it ten times. Twenty times. Fifty times.

His arms were tired. His hands were sore. He did not stop.

He thought about Lysette. She had danced with him. She had taught him. She had smiled. A small smile. But real.

He practiced the Latch. The shield catches a blade and locks it against the knocker ring. He did it ten times. Twenty times.

He thought about the roof. The bench. The wind. The plants. The city. Lysette beside him. The warmth.

He stopped. He put down his lance. He sat on the bench. He breathed.

He thought about the feeling in his chest. Warm. Small. He did not name it. He did not need to. But it was there. And it was not going away.

He picked up his lance. He practiced one more time. Then he stopped. He went to the dining hall.

He ate lunch. He sat alone. He did not taste the food. He thought about Lysette. He thought about the warmth.

After lunch, he went to the library. He looked for books about the Ashen Crowns. He found one. It was old. The cover was red.

He opened it. The pages were yellow. The writing was small. He read.

"The Ashen Crowns are a terror network. They are the heirs of the eleven kingdoms conquered by the Iron King. They seek revenge. They use Veils. They use bombs. They use fear."

Eden read this. He knew about the Ashen Crowns. But the book gave him details. Dates. Names. Places.

He wrote in his notebook: "Ashen Crowns. Heirs of conquered kingdoms. Revenge. Use Veils, bombs, fear. Read more."

He closed the book. He put it back. He would come back tomorrow.

He went to the roof garden. He sat on the bench. He looked at the city. The lamps glowed. The trams ran. The city was normal.

But the city was not safe. The Veils were in the walls. The lamps were their eyes. The clerk was watching. The teacher was watching. The Ashen Crowns were watching.

Eden watched back. He always watched back.

He closed his notebook. He lay back on the bench. He looked at the sky. The clouds moved. The wind blew.

He thought about Lysette. The warmth. The dance. The smile.

He slept on the roof. No dreams.
