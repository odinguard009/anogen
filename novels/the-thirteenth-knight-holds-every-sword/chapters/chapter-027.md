# Chapter 27: The Paper

Eden found a paper. It was on the floor of the library. Under a shelf. It was old. Yellow. The edges were torn.

He picked it up. It had writing. Old writing. Not English. Not the symbols from the lamp house. Something else.

He took it to his room. He sat on his bed. He looked at it. The writing was small. Careful. Each letter was perfect.

He could not read it. He did not know the language. But he knew someone who might.

He went to the academy's language teacher. Her name was Miss Aldous. She was old. She had white hair. She wore glasses.

Eden showed her the paper. She put on her glasses. She looked at it. She was quiet for a long time.

"Where did you find this?" she asked.

"In the library. Under a shelf."

Miss Aldous looked at him. "This is not a language. This is a code. A very old code. I have seen it before. In the archives. In the basement of the academy."

"Can you read it?" Eden asked.

"No. But I know who can. The archivist. He is in the basement. He has been there for forty years."

Eden went to the basement. The archivist was a small man. He had a beard. He wore a coat with many pockets. He was surrounded by books and papers.

Eden showed him the paper. The archivist put on glasses. He looked at it. He was quiet.

"This is a message," the archivist said. "It is from the Thirteenth Vigil."

Eden's heart stopped. "The Thirteenth Vigil?"

"Yes. I have seen this code before. It was used by the Thirteenth Vigil. Long ago. Before the academy was built."

"What does it say?"

The archivist read it. Slowly. "It says: 'The Veils are in the walls. The lamps are their eyes. Do not trust the light.'"

Eden took the paper. He folded it. He put it in his pocket.

"Thank you," he said.

He left. He went to the roof garden. He sat on the bench. He read the paper again.

"The Veils are in the walls. The lamps are their eyes. Do not trust the light."

The lamps. The lamp houses. The clerk. The teacher. The dye-works. They were all connected. The Veils were in the walls. The lamps were their eyes.

Eden thought about this. The lamps in the city. They were not just lamps. They were eyes. Veils could see through them. Or control them. Or both.

He had stopped the clerk. But there were other lamps. Other eyes.

He wrote in his notebook: "Thirteenth Vigil code. Veils in walls. Lamps are eyes. Do not trust light. Check all lamps."

He closed the notebook. He looked at the city. The lamps glowed. They looked normal. But they were not.

He would check every lamp. Every lamp house. Every wire.

He would not trust the light.
