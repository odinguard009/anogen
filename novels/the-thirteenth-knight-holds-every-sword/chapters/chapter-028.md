# Chapter 28: Silverfell

Silverfell was a town. Small. Two hours by train from Solmarch. It was in the hills. Green hills. Sheep. Stone walls.

Eden went with a group. Twenty students. A teacher. A field trip. They were studying the old walls. The walls were from before the invasion. Before the Pale. Before everything.

Eden did not care about the walls. He cared about the town. Silverfell was near a lamp house. Lamp House 11. He had seen it on the list.

The group walked through the town. The teacher talked. Eden did not listen. He looked at the buildings. He looked at the people. He looked for Veils.

A woman stood at a window. She watched the group. Her shadow did not breathe. Eden saw it. He stopped.

"Eden?" Lysette said. She was beside him. "What is wrong?"

"The woman. In the window. Her shadow."

Lysette looked. The woman's shadow was flat. Still. Not breathing.

"Veil," Lysette said.

"Not here," Eden said. "Too many people."

They walked on. The group left the town. They went to the walls. Eden stayed at the back. He watched the town. The woman was gone from the window.

After the field trip, the group went home. Eden stayed. He told the teacher he was sick. The teacher let him stay.

Eden waited. Night came. The lamps in Silverfell turned on. He watched them. They looked normal.

He went to Lamp House 11. It was on a hill. Like the others. He unlocked the door. Inside, the machines were running. The switch was untouched.

But there were papers. New papers. Eden read them. They were orders. For Lamp House 11. Turn off the lamp. Friday. 8 PM. Same as the others.

But Friday had passed. The lamps had not been turned off. Because Eden had stopped them. The Ashen Crowns knew. They would try again.

Eden took the papers. He left. He went back to the town. He found the woman. She was at the window again. Her shadow did not breathe.

He did not go to her. He watched. He counted. He waited.

At midnight, she left the building. She walked to the lamp house. Eden followed. She went inside. He waited outside.

She came out ten minutes later. She walked back to the building. Eden went inside. The switch was untouched. But the papers were gone. She had taken them.

Eden left. He went to the train station. He took the night train back to Solmarch.

He sat in the dark. The train rocked. He thought about Silverfell. One more lamp house. One more Veil. One more piece of the puzzle.

He wrote in his notebook: "Silverfell. Lamp House 11. Veil in window. She took the papers. She knows we are watching."

He closed the notebook. He looked out the window. The dark hills passed. The lamps of small towns glowed in the distance.

He did not trust the light. He would never trust the light again.
