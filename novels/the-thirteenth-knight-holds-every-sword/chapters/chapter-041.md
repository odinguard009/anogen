# Chapter 41: Under the Wall

The first night Eden went down through the oil line behind the stores and came up three hours before dawn with grey dust in the seams of his coat. And Lysette was at the top of the stair when he came in. She had a round sheet in her hand and a pencil behind her ear. She looked at his boots and then at his face.

"You were on duty until the second bell," she said.

"Yes."

"You are not on duty now."

"I could not sleep."

"Then you could not sleep somewhere else."

Eden took off his coat and hung it on a peg. He turned the pocket that held the notebook away from her because there was grey dust on the cloth. She saw him do it, and she wrote a line in her book. "I will write that down," she said.

"Write it," Eden said, and he went to his bed and lay down with his boots on, and he slept until the bell woke him, and she was there again with the round sheet, and she had written four lines about him since the morning.

Rowan asked him at breakfast why he was sleeping in his boots. And Eden said that he liked them. And Rowan said that nobody liked boots that much. And Clara said that some people did and that he should leave it alone. Felix put a bun on the table in front of Eden and asked whether he had eaten. And Eden said yes, and Felix said that was a lie told in a calm voice, and Eden said it was the only voice he had. Lysette wrote a line in her book and did not look up. And Eden knew that she had heard Felix and had decided not to ask.

For three nights he did the same thing. He slept in the camp under Lysette's eyes and went down under the Wall when the camp was quiet. He came back with his boots grey and his coat smelling of lime. Each time she wrote a line and said nothing else. Each time he let her.

Vesk led him down through a gap behind the fourth lamp. The shadow of the lamp fell on a crack in the stone. The crack opened when Vesk knocked twice. And Eden went in with his shield across his chest. The stair went down for a long time. The air changed from cold to damp. The damp had a smell that Eden could not place at first. Then he placed it, and it was the smell of a cellar that had been closed for years with something living in it.

The tunnel was not a tunnel in and a cellar was a tunnel. It had walls that breathed, and the stone moved a little when he put his hand on it, and the air had a smell like wet iron and old bread, and Vesk walked ahead in a shadow that kept the shape of a tall man with a limp.

"It is a living thing," Vesk said. He said it from the dark where his small body was hidden. "The old maps call it a road, and it is not a road. It is a mouth that has been here longer than the Wall."

"Where does it end?"

"Under the camp, and then further south than the maps say. Vesk's coat dragged on the stone. "Your oil lines are good. They give me an edge to walk in."

Eden had laid the lines two days before the first night. Painting dark oil under beams and along the sill of empty stores. The lamp men had walked past them without looking twice. The lines held shadows in the morning light. The shadows were openings waiting for a knock. He marked the mouth of the tunnel on a piece of paper with a pencil. He marked the turn where the stone breathed hardest. He put the paper in his boot because the camp was searched in the mornings.

Eden marked the turn where the wall breathed hardest. He put a small scratch on the stone with his spear point so that he would find it in the dark. And Vesk watched him do it and said that a mark was a promise to come back. That some promises were better not made. Eden said nothing, and he wrote the turn in his notebook, and he put the notebook inside his coat where the sweat would not reach it.

On the second night he found gloves.

They lay on a flat stone in a side passage. They were grey work gloves with the fingers curled and a hand had just taken them off. There were grey veins on the leather where the skin had touched it. Eden picked one up with the tip of his spear and turned it over. The inside was warm.

"Grafted," Vesk said. "Not a Veil. A person wearing Hush under the skin. Doing it recently, because the leather has not gone hard yet."

"Children?"

"People. Vesk did not say more, and Eden put the glove back on the stone, and he took a scraping of the grey from the leather into a tin, and he wrote one word in his notebook: people.

On the third night he saw the boot print.

It was in the grey dust of the tunnel floor. Deep and wide, with a row of small nails in the heel and a ridge across the toe that had been worn down on one side, and the print was going south when the camp was behind them and north when he looked at it again, and Eden crouched over it with his lamp held low.

He put his glove near the print without touching it. The print was longer than his hand and deeper at the toe. The nails in the heel had left little round marks in the grey. There were nine of them on one side and eight on the other because one nail was missing. He counted them twice, and he counted them a third time because the number mattered, and then he covered the print with his boot so that Vesk would not step on it, and he waited until the little man had gone past.

A grounds man's boot, he thought, and he did not write it down.

Felix looked at the grey in the tin when Eden showed it to him. He did not ask where it had come from. He said that the grey had been on the glove in a thin line. Like a man had touched it and then taken his hand away quickly. That was a different thing from a Husk mark, and Eden said that he knew. Felix said that if Eden ever wanted a second pair of eyes down there he should ask. And Eden said that he would. And Felix said that he knew he would not. That he would come anyway if the third night turned into a fourth.

Before the fourth night Okoru called spear Nine to the stores and handed out sealed gloves and hoods. The gloves were ward-plate with taped joints and a folded second cuff. The hoods covered the neck and fastened under the chin. No skin showed anywhere when they were done.

"A drop of Husk blood on an unsealed cuff will grey the leather along the seam in a few hours," Okoru said. He held up a strip of leather with a grey line running through it to show them. "Dead grey stays active for four hours. Do not touch it, and do not let it touch you. The nest is under this camp, and you are going to look at it sealed."

A conscript at the back had come in late and had not been given a hood. His cuff was open at the wrist. He stood with his hands behind him while Okoru spoke. When a veteran dropped a jar of grey dust beside him. A spot of it went onto the leather. The conscript slapped his cuff and went pale. The grey spread up the seam while they watched. He did not make a sound.

Okoru sent him to the infirmary with two men. He told the rest of them to seal their cuffs twice. And Eden watched the strip of leather in Okoru's hand. The grey line on it had grown longer while he looked at it. He pulled his own cuff down over his glove and tied the second fold.

Deep in the tunnel on the fourth night. Eden put his hand on the breathing wall and tried to read the next turn and he read a lamp shadow. For one heartbeat the reading went blank. And there was no line in his mind and no shape in the dark. and someone on the other side of the wall had looked away from him and closed a door.

He stood still with his hand on the stone until the reading came back. And Vesk said nothing, and the dark ahead of them kept its shape, and Eden drew a breath and went on, and he did not tell Vesk that for one heartbeat he had not known where he was.

They came back at dawn, and the camp was waking, and the grounds man was in the yard with a broom and a pair of grey boots on his feet, and he hummed a tune with four notes down and one that went up, and he waved at Eden and he had been expecting him.

Eden looked at the boots, and then at the nails in the heels, and then at the ridge worn down on one side, and he went past with his hands in his pockets and his gloves sealed under his coat.

The infirmary sent the conscript back at noon with his cuff cut off and a grey bandage on his wrist. And he sat on the step outside the stores and held his hand in his lap like a thing that belonged to someone else. Eden came past with a tin of oil. And the conscript looked up and asked whether it was true that dead grey stayed active for four hours. And Eden said that it was true. And the conscript asked whether it was true that it kept going after that. And Eden said that he did not know, and that the sergeant had said four hours because that was the number he used.

"Will it reach my heart? the conscript asked.

"Not in four hours," Eden said.

"That is what the healer said. The boy looked at the bandage and then at the sky. "I have a mother in the south. "And I wrote her a letter this morning. "And I did not tell her about this, and now I am glad I did not."

Eden stood with the tin in his hand and did not say anything. Because there was no sentence that would make the boy's hand better. And he walked on to the stores and put the oil on the beam. And the line came out straight again. And he had to stop and rest his hand twice before it was done.

That evening Rowan found the mark on the stone at the turn and asked what it was. And Eden said it was a scratch. And Rowan said that it looked like a door mark. And Eden said that it looked like a scratch. And Rowan shrugged and went back to the camp. Felix came later and stood over the mark with his hands in his pockets. And he said that it was a door mark. And that he had seen Eden lay three of them in the camp. And that he did not need to know where they went. Then he put a peppermint on the edge of the stone and walked away. And Eden took the peppermint and ate it. And it was the last sweet thing he tasted for a week.
