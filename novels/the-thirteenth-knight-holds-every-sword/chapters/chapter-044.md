# Chapter 44: Warm Stone

The dream came with a table that had not been there before. The Dragon stood on the far side of it with a shield in its hand. The shield was round and iron-faced and had a worn strap.

"Thessa," the Dragon said, and it put the shield on the table so that Eden could see the small mark under the strap, which was a feather no bigger than a nail.

Eden knew the mark, and he knew the shield, and he had held it once with a name spoken through a knock on a ring. He had given it back and never asked who made it.

"Whose hand mended it? he asked.

"Mended? the Dragon said, and its face went still in the way a face goes still when a thing has been said wrong. "Nothing about that shield has been mended. It came out of a fire whole."

"Then who made the feather?"

The Dragon looked at the shield and then at EdenIt said nothing. The table went white, and the dream ended with the sound of a door closing somewhere under the floor.

Eden woke with his hand closed on his coat and the cold coming through the camp wall. He lay still for a while with the mark of the feather in his mind. Because a thing that had come out of a fire whole was not the same as a thing that had never been broken. The Dragon had known the difference and had not wanted to say it.

At breakfast Rowan asked Eden whether he had slept. And Eden said that he had. And Rowan said that he had dreamed about a shield. And Eden put his spoon down before he could stop himself, and Rowan saw it. "You did too," Rowan said. "You always put the spoon down when a dream means something. Eden said that he had put the spoon down because it was wet. And Rowan said that a wet spoon did not make a person stop eating. He let it go because Clara had told him to let things go. Felix watched both of them and said nothing. He put a bun beside Eden's cup and pushed it across the table with one finger.

At the morning briefing Okoru pointed at a map of the supply tunnels and told the group to go through them in pairs. He said that the dark tunnels were old and that the Wall people used them for stores. That a person could walk from one end of the camp to the other without ever coming up into the light. Lysette wrote the route in her book and asked why they were going down. And Okoru said that a thing under the camp had been listening. That a thing that listened would be looked at. That the five of them were small enough to look without being seen.

The supply stair was behind the coal stores and had a door that stuck in the cold. And Okoru pushed it open with his stick and told them to go one at a time and not to touch the walls. Because the old tunnels held grey dust that could make a person ill. Lysette wrote that down, and Clara repeated it to Rowan. He said he had heard it the first time, and she said he had not. He had been listening with half his mind, and she said the other half had better start, and he went down first with his light.

They went down at the second bell with their lamps low and their hoods fastened. The tunnels were dry and narrow. The stone had a thin coat of grey dust that showed every boot print. Felix went first with his bow across his chest. He stopped at every turn and listened. Then he knelt and put one hand flat on the ground.

"Trail," he said, and a pale line came up out of the grey dust and ran along the tunnel toward the next turn. It showed a boot going south and then coming back. It stopped where the stone had been swept clean. It started again beyond the sweep, going south.

"Someone swept it," Lysette said.

"Someone swept one patch," Felix said. "That is a shorter job than sweeping the whole tunnel."

Rowan's light came up in his palms. The light was apple-sized and soft, and he held it low so it lit the floor and not the ceiling. The pale line showed clearly in the gold, and the boot print in the grey dust was broad with a row of small nails in the heel. He put his own boot beside it and looked. His boot was smaller by two finger widths.

"grounds man," he said, and he said it only to Felix.

The tunnel turned and went down. The grey dust on the floor got thinner. The stone under their boots began to feel different. And Rowan said that the wall was warm. And Clara put her hand on it and said that it was warmer than the wall above. And Eden put his glove against the stone and felt a faint heat coming up through the leather. He did not say what he thought it meant.

A sound came from ahead, and it was the sound of boots on grey dust, and it stopped when Felix held up his hand. The five of them stood without moving. The dark in front of them was empty. And Eden saw two shadows that were not their own move across the far wall and then go out. He did not see the men who cast them.

"Watchers," Felix breathed, and he put an arrow to his string without a sound.

A shape stepped into the pale line of the **[Trail]** ahead of them. It was a man in a grey coat with a grey band at his cuff. It had a blade in its right hand and a graft that showed under the skin of its neck. It came on without hurry because it had not seen them or did not care. Felix knelt with one knee on the stone and shut his eyes. He held the arrow at his cheek and waited. The tunnel went so quiet that Eden could hear the blood in his own ears.

"**[Osk]**," Felix said, and the arrow went down the path that the Trail heard rather than the path the eye saw. It made no sound, and the man in the grey coat stopped with his hand half raised, and then he fell forward into the grey dust without a cry, and the grey at his neck went still.

"One arrow," Felix said, and he opened his eyes and took another arrow from his quiver and put it back, because there was no second shot in it, and he knew it.

Lysette looked at the fallen man and then at the arrow. She said that a shot with no sound was a useful thing in a tunnel. And Felix said that it was useful once. That he had one arrow and no second shot. She wrote that down in her book and said that a limit was a thing worth writing. Eden looked at the grey on the man's neck and saw that the veins had stopped where the arrow had gone in. The grey beyond that point had gone dark. He did not touch it. He told the others to step around the body without stepping in the grey dust.

They went on, and the tunnel turned again, and Rowan was at the front with his light low. A loose piece of stone shifted under his boot and slid down the passage. Lysette stepped forward in the dark and grabbed Eden's sleeve to stop him from following, and her fingers stayed on the cloth for three breaths before she let go, and neither of them spoke.

Clara stopped and put her staff across the passage. She listened, and then she said the watchers were gone. Eden looked at the floor where the grey dust was thin, and the stone beneath it was warm enough that the grey dust had dried and lifted in small curls, and he put his glove down again.

"Mawgates are warm before they open," Vesk said from the shadow behind him. His voice was flat and low. "I have felt it once before. "And that gate was under a city, and the city is not there now."

They came back up through the coal stores at dawn. The sky was grey and the camp was beginning to stir. The grounds man was at the pump with his sleeves rolled up and a bucket in his hand. He looked at their hoods and then at their boots. He said that the old tunnels were no place for young people. That he had lost a cousin down there in the first winter and had never gone back. That the cousin had been a good man with a bad habit of touching walls. Rowan asked which wall, and the grounds man pointed at the coal stores with his bucket and said that it was the one they had just come out of, and he went back to pumping with his face open and friendly, and he hummed four notes down and one up while he worked.

Eden stood at the top of the stair and watched the man's shadow on the ground. The shadow moved when he moved. It kept the shape of the bucket and the sleeves. It did not move when his chest moved with the tune. And Eden took his hand out of his pocket and put it on the wall beside the stair. The wall was warm there too. The warmth ran along the stone toward the pump and the stone were listening to the man and waiting for him to finish.

Felix came up beside him and put one boot on the lower step. He said that he had checked the oil lines again and that they were dry and clean. That a man who laid oil days ahead of a thing was a man who expected the thing to happen. And Eden said that he had expected it. And Felix said that he knew. He asked whether Eden had a plan for when it did. And Eden said that he had a plan. And Felix said that he was glad. Then he went into the mess hut and asked for a bun and the whole conversation had been about breakfast.

Lysette wrote one line in her book at the breakfast table. She wrote another line under it, and the second line said: warm before it opens. She did not show the lines to anyone. She put the book in her coat and went to her post. When Rowan asked her what she had written she said that it was a note about the coal stores and that the coal stores were a disgrace. Which was true, and Rowan believed her, and went to stand beside Clara with his one glove on his hand and the other in his pocket.

Eden lay awake that night and went over the shield in the dream again. The strap had been worn where a thumb had held it. The feather under it was small. And the Dragon had said that nothing about it had been mended. And he had known that was not the same as the truth. He had seen a man turn a button on his cuff in a warm room in December. And he had seen the grey in a tunnel stop at an arrow. And these were two facts that belonged in the same file. And he could not put them there because the file had a missing page and the missing page was the one that would have joined them.

"Did you sleep? Felix asked at breakfast.

"Some."

"Did you dream?"

"Yes."

"Was it a good dream?"

"I do not know."

Felix put his bun down and looked at the table. And he said that he had had a dream too. And that in his dream he had been pushing a cart up a road. And that the cart had a wheel that would not turn. And that he had been alone with it. Eden said that a bad wheel was a thing a person could fix. And Felix said that he had never thought of it that way. And then he said that he would think about it. And he ate his bun and did not ask another question.
