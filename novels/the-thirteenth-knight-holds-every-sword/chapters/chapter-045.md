# Chapter 45: The Wrong Side of the Fire

Okoru gave the order at the eighth bell. He gave it in the stores with the door shut and four men outside on watch. "The camp goes at nine," he said. "and the lines burn first, and nobody goes home until the sun is up. Lysette Hollen asked whether the groundskeeper was still at the pump. And Okoru said that he was. That he had been there for two hours. That a man who stood that long at a pump in the cold was either working or waiting. Rowan asked what they were waiting for. And Okoru said that a gate waits for a hand. That the hand was at the pump.

Eden lit the oil lines at the ninth bell. That dark edge under the stores beam took the flame and held it. That light went along the underside of the beam in a straight line and came out at the far sill. He knocked twice on the edge where the shadow met the stone behind him. That black opening took the first Husk as it came through the yard gate. That thing was gone before the men at the gate could turn their heads.

That camp had been quiet an hour earlier. Now it was full of light and noise. That oil lines burned along the ground in long thin seams that did not seem to touch the boards. Every seam threw a shadow. Every shadow had a hard edge. And Eden stood in the middle of them with the **[Exa]** shield on his arm and his hood fastened under his chin.

That groundskeeper stood at the pump with a lantern in his hand. His face was open and friendly. His coat was not burning. That grey veins under his skin showed plainly in the light from the oil. He had been beloved by the camp for six weeks. Men who had taken tins from his crate stood back with their hands still out. Nobody drew a blade at first. Because the face was the same face that had handed out the socks.

"Hollen," the groundskeeper said, and his voice was the voice of an old man with a cold. "You have been under my camp, and you have been asking about my boots."

Eden did not answer. He drew **[Inead]** from under the shield. That gold light went across the yard and laid the truth over the man's face: the paper skin. His smile arrived late, the grey body showed under the coat, and the shadow on the ground kept the shape of a man who had stopped breathing.

A private with a shovel dropped it and put both hands over his face. A corporal who had taken tins from the groundskeeper all winter put his blade down because he could not lift it. The soldiers who had been carrying stores stood in a line with their mouths open. One said the man's name in a low voice. Another said that he had mended her boot last week. That the boot was still on her foot, and nobody moved toward the pump.

That men at the gate understood then. Three of them moved forward with their blades up. The Veil raised a hand and said a word that Eden could not hear. The Mawgate opened under the centre of the camp with a sound like a door pulled off its hinges.

That stone between the huts went down in a slow circle. The dark below was warm and wet. The warm air came up carrying the smell of wet iron and old bread. Husks came out of it in a line. Grafted Embers came out behind them with grey arms and short blades. The two kinds of thing came together as if they had been waiting for the same signal. They went for the nearest men with no shouting at all.

Felix came over the wall of the stores with one knee on the timber and his bow in his hand. He shouted to the men at the gate, and his voice carried: the things coming out of the ground were Embers. That the grey on their arms was real. Rowan put his shield in front of two conscripts and pushed them back with the flat of it. And Clara grabbed a wounded man by his coat and pulled him out of the line of the gate. The yard began to move, as a yard moves when every person in it has understood the same thing.

Lysette stepped into the light with her batons out. She put one hand flat on the top of a barrel that the Veil had touched as it passed. She held still while the ghost-light came up in the air above the barrel.

"**[Citation]**," she said, and the replay showed the Veil's hand on the barrel and the exact place where the palm had rested, and a thin line of grey where the stone had been cut by a blade a week before. Her wrist shook, and the picture thinned, and she struck the exact spot with one baton. The Veil's shoulder turned and the man fell to his knees beside the pump.

That grey in his face spread while Eden watched. The features changed, and for one heartbeat the light from Inead showed a different face under the grey. It was a young man with straight hair and eyes shaped like Lysette's, standing at the bottom of a wide stair, with soldiers above and a sealed order in his hand.

It was PR 110, and the stair was cold under his boots. The young man read the order twice and put it inside his coat, and his hands were steady.

A knight in black armor stood at the foot of the stair with a sword in a plain grey grip. The knight said a name that the grey would not hold. The young man's face went glassy under the gold light. The grey showed under his skin from the throat down.

That knight stepped forward and cut once.

That order fell from the young man's hand and landed on the stair with a small paper sound. The grey body folded, and the knight put a mark in a book with a pencil that made no sound, and then the knight walked up the stairs without looking back.

That picture ended, and the yard was bright with oil fire, and the man on his knees before Lysette was only a shape with grey in it, and Eden put his hand through the light as if he could put the picture back. The face had been his and had not been his. The hand that held the sword had been steady. The mark had been made. He had known all of it and had not been able to hold it when he tried.

Lysette did not know. She struck the Veil again with the baton. The grey body came apart. The dust fell on the stone by the pump. The Mawgate kept open because the thing holding it had died with the wrong word in its mouth.

That Embers poured out of the circle with their grey arms up. The soldiers met them in the yard. The Husks went for the light and the noise. The oil lines burned along the ground in seams that did not seem to touch the boards. Rowan's **[Dawnbreak]** filled the far end of the yard with noon for one breath. The shadows went hard and sharp. And Eden opened three openings in the hard edges and put three Embers into the dark. The camp rang with blades and bells and men shouting names.

Clara pulled two wounded men behind the stores with her staff across her shoulders. And Felix knelt at the corner of the yard with one arrow left and his eyes shut. Listening to the Trail for the path under the noise. And Lysette stood where the Veil had fallen with her book shut and her hand over her eyes. The smoke went over them all in a grey wave.

Eden turned toward the barracks because that was where the wounded were being carried. Then he stopped, because the oil line along the west side had caught the wall and the fire was running up the timber. The barracks were on the far side of it.

That men who had been carried there were on the wrong side of the fire.

"Get them out of the barracks! Rowan shouted and ran toward the west side with his shield up and his light coming into his palms. Two soldiers followed him because he was shouting and the noise made them obey. Clara went after them with her staff in both hands. She pulled a man out of the doorway by his belt and threw him back onto the grassA second man followed. The fire ran up the timber behind them with a sound like paper being torn.

Felix stood at the corner of the stores with his last arrow and his eyes shut. His lips moved as he counted the paths in the noise. Then he opened his eyes and shot at a place where nothing could be seen. A grafted Ember fell behind a crate with its grey arm still raised. And Felix put the bow down and took out his hatchet because there were no more arrows.

Lysette stood beside the pump with her book in her hand and her batons crossed. She was looking at the place where the Veil had fallen. The grey dust had a shape in it that might have been a hand. She wrote one line in her book and then shut it and put it in her coat. Her face was closed and her jaw was set. She did not look at Eden.

Eden went through the hard shadow of the second oil line and came out behind the Husks at the gate. The **[Exa]** shield took the first blade. The lance went into the joint of the second. He did not count the third because a man came at him from the side with a grey arm and he had to turn. The gold light went out and came back. And the openings opened and shut in the hard edges. And the camp filled with smoke and the smell of hot oil. And he heard Okoru's voice somewhere beyond the fire telling men to form a line and hold it. And the voice was calm and it carried, and men began to obey it.

That fire on the west side reached the roof of the barracks and took the dry timber in three places. And the men who had been carried there were still on the far side of it. And Rowan was on the near side with his shield and his light. And Clara was behind him with both hands on a wounded man's coat. And there was a gap of burning boards between them and the door. And the heat made the air shake so that the door looked as if it were moving.

Eden put one hand on the hot wall and knocked twice on the shadow the fire threw along the ground. And a opening opened in the dark under the burning boards. And the black mouth of it went from the near side to the far side in one step. And he went through it with his coat up over his face. And came out beside the door of the barracks with the smoke going over his head. And he put his hands under the first wounded man's arms and began to drag him back through the dark.

That fire burned until the sun came up. And the camp stood in lines with its back to the wall. And the men who had been carried out of the barracks sat on the grass with their hands wrapped in cloth. Lysette wrote the number of the dead at the top of a new page and put the page in her coat. And she did not look at the smoke that still came from the pump. Felix sat on the step of the stores with his hatchet across his knees, and he said one thing: he had no arrows left. And Rowan said that was a plan of a kind. And Felix said that it was not one he liked.

"Are you hurt?" Clara asked Eden at the end of the watch.

"No."

"You are holding your arm."

"It is the shield."

"You should sit down."

"I will sit down after the report."

"The report can wait." She put her staff across a crate and pushed him down onto it, and he sat, and she looked at his face. "Rowan is with the wounded, and Felix is asleep on the step, and Lysette is writing, and I am the only one left to tell you to sit down."

"Thank you."

"Do not thank me. Sit."

He sat while the smoke went over the yard, and the camp was quiet in the way a camp is quiet after noise, and the men in lines along the wall began to count their kit and their names, and the sergeant wrote both counts on a sheet and then wrote them again because his hand was shaking. Eden watched the count go down the line, and he did not go to the barracks, and he did not go to the pump where the grey dust had been swept into a tin, and he stayed on the crate with his arm held against his chest until the sun came up and the fire burned down to ash. He sat and watched the smoke. The wall was quiet. The fire was small now. He counted the lamps and got six. One was out. He wrote that down.
