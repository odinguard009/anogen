# Chapter 46: The Lane

The camp cook met Eden at the kitchen step with a ladle in her hand. Her apron was singed at one corner. "They are coming up the north lane," she said. "Forty people are behind me, and I have counted them twice because I wanted the number to be true. Eden asked her to keep the people on the step and not let them run. She said that she had been telling them that for ten minutes and that a person who ran in a yard full of fire was a person who fell down. Then she called a number in a loud voice and the people behind her stopped moving.

The supply wagon stood in the north lane with its back wheel in a rut. Its shadow lay long across the stone because the fire was burning behind it. And Eden put his hand on the tailboard and held the wagon still.

"Hold it," he said to the driver. The driver held the reins with both hands and did not ask why.

The Husks came up the north lane in a line. Their long hands scraped the wall as they ran. Two Embers followed them with muskets that cracked once and then went quiet because the mana haze from the fire had fouled the locks. A man fell beside the kitchen step. And Okoru was beyond him with his stick in his hand and his arm up. Cut off from the rest of the yard by three Husks and a wall of smoke.

Lysette came up behind him with her round sheet in her hand and said the numbers again: forty-one at the gate. Forty on the step, and one still walking somewhere in the smoke. Rowan had his light in his palms and his shield on his arm. His face was set because he had been told to hold and holding was what he did. Felix was already at the corner with an arrow at his cheek. And Clara had her staff across her shoulders and her mouth shut. And Eden saw all of them in one look and knew that he did not have to give another order.

"the group," Eden said, and the five of them moved.

The wagon's shadow was hard and clean under the wheel. And Eden tapped twice on the edge of it and the dark took the first three Husks before they reached the step. The fourth turned and ran into the fifth. The lane made a sound like a door shutting in a deep house. Felix stood at the corner with an arrow at his cheek and his eyes on the yard. He did not shoot, because the wagon had done the work.

The cook stood on the kitchen step with a ladle in her hand and counted the people coming past her. She called the numbers aloud because she had always counted that way. When she got to forty she stopped and called the number again. Because the yard was still full of smoke and she did not trust her eyes.

"Forty," she said, and then she said it again. "Forty out."

Eden left the wagon shadow shut and went along the step to where the Husks had broken through. The yard was hot on one side and cold on the other. The smoke made the men at the gate look like shapes cut out of paper. Lysette was at the step with her batons out. She had a round sheet in her hand and a line of numbers on it. She said that there were forty-one people in the camp when the gate opened and that she had counted them all. That one was still missing.

Then Rowan's light came up through the smoke.

It did not come up apple-sized and soft. It came up his arms and out through the spear head in one hard burst. The gold filled the space around the kitchen step bright as noon. The shadows of the Husks went black and sharp on the stone. And Eden felt the heat of it on his face even through his hood.

"**[Ithe]**," Rowan said, and his voice cracked on the word.

The **[Ithe]** was a burst of gold from the spear head that drove the Hushborn back and threw hard shadows. It spent the warmth in his hands and left him cold. After one burst his hands did not always answer when he told them to. The Husks at the step went back three paces and then fell. The light went out of Rowan's arms and a mouth had closed over it. He staggered and put a hand on the wall. His face had gone the colour of ash.

Clara was beside him before he fell. She put her staff across the crack in the stone and held it thereA green line ran out along the crack and up the wall. The Husks on the far side of it stopped and they had come to the edge of a river they could not cross.

"**[Wyl]**," Clara said, and her voice was low and steady.

The **[Wyl]** was a green line along a crack that held the Hushborn on the far side. Every blow struck on the line came back into her. She felt each one in her face and in the hand that held the staff. She took the first blow through the staff and her head went back. She took the second and blood came under her nose. The line held, and the grass in the crack where her knees had been went green for one second in the middle of winter, and then it was grey again.

Eden saw the grass and wrote nothing down. Because there was no plain word for a thing that happened for one second in a wall crack while a woman held a line with her blood.

Okoru fought his way out of the smoke with his stick in his hand and a cut on his sleeve. He came to the step and said that the yard was lost and that the people had to go out through the south gate in lines of ten. And Lysette said that she had the list and would call the names. And Okoru said that a list was a good thing and that he had been right to teach her to keep one. Then he went back into the smoke because there were men still inside it. The cook hit an Ember in the wrist with her ladle and told it to leave her kitchen alone.

Valerius came through the south gate with the other group behind him. They were late and they were furious. His white band was covered in soot. He stopped in the middle of the yard and looked at the burning camp. The air around his raised shoulder frosted over for one breath and then went clear. The men near him stepped back because they had seen it or because they had felt it. And Eden had seen it and kept his face still.

"Who held this yard? Valerius asked, and nobody answered, and he looked at the wagon in the lane and the black mouth of the shadow under it, and at the green line by the step, and at Rowan sitting with his back against the wall with his hands in his lap, and he said nothing else.

An Ember with a grey arm came at the cook from the smoke. And Clara pulled the staff up to turn the line. The cost of it went through her face. She dropped to one knee. The cook swung the ladle and hit the Ember in the wrist. Which was not enough and was all she had.

A conscript boy came running out of the barracks with his coat on fire at one sleeve. He was small and his face was wet. He was heading the wrong way because the gate was behind him and the fire was in front of him. He ran straight past Eden toward the north lane where the Husks had been.

Clara saw him first, and she got up from her knee with blood on her lip and the staff in both hands, and she ran after him into the smoke.

"Clara! Rowan shouted, and he tried to stand, and his hands did not answer him, and he fell back against the wall.

The south gate took the people in groups of ten. And Lysette called the names from her sheet. The cook checked each face before she let a group go. She did it with her ladle held across the gate like a bar. A man argued with her because he wanted to go back for his kit. She hit him on the shoulder with the ladle and told him that a kit was a bag and that a bag could burn. He went out, and she called the next ten.

Felix came back to the step with his bow in his hand and his face white. He said that an Ember with a musket had fired three times at the gate and that the third shot had gone wide because the lock had fouled. That the mana haze was thick enough to spoil a gun. Eden asked whether Felix had been hit. And Felix said no, and then he said that he had thrown his bow at the man's head from the wall, which was not a thing an archer should do, and that the man had gone down anyway, and he put the bow over his shoulder and looked at the ground.

Rowan sat against the wall with his hands in his lap. They did not move when he told them to. He said that it was a strange feeling. Like hands that belonged to a friend who had walked away. Clara knelt beside him and put her staff across her knees. She said that it would come back. He said that he knew it would. That knowing was not the same as having it. She said nothing to that because it was true.

Lysette finished the list and put the sheet in her coat. She went to the edge of the yard and looked at the smoke. She said that the number was forty and that the number was right. And that she had counted every person out and none of them back in. And Eden stood beside her and said that one of the forty was still in the barracks. And she said that she knew. And that she had left the name off the sheet because she had not known whether to write it as living or dead. He did not answer, and she wrote the name on a clean corner of the sheet and did not put a mark beside it.

"How many went out? Rowan asked from the wall.

"Forty," Lysette said. "I counted them and the cook counted them, and we got the same number."

"Was the boy in the forty?"

"I do not know. She turned the sheet and looked at the clean corner. "His name is here, but I did not put a mark beside it, because I did not see him go out, and I do not write a living where I have not seen one."

Clara came back through the smoke with her staff in one hand and blood on her lip. And she said that the boy had been in the barracks and that she had seen him running and that she had gone after him. And Rowan asked whether she had caught him. And she said that she had not. And that the smoke had taken him. And she put a hand on the wall and stood very still. And Eden saw her hand shaking where it touched the stone.

"Then we go in," Eden said. And he took the shield off his arm and put it back because he needed both hands free for the dark. And Lysette said that she was coming. And Felix said that he was coming. And Rowan tried to stand and fell back. And Clara said that he would stay where he was and that this was not a thing he could do with hands that did not answer. And he said nothing, and that was the first time Eden had heard him agree when he did not want to.

"Where is the boy? Lysette asked.

"In the smoke."

"Did you see him?"

"For one step."

"Which way did he go?"

"North. Eden pointed at the lane, and his hand was steady, and he put it down. "He was running north, and Clara went after him, and then the smoke came between us."

Felix put his bow on the wall and said that he had been up on the stores and had seen the boy. And Lysette asked whether he was hurt. And Felix said that the boy's sleeve was burning and that he was running with his hands over his face. And that a boy who ran with his hands over his face was a boy who could not see where he was going. And that this was the part that worried him. Clara put a hand on the wall and said that she had told him to stop. And that he had not stopped. And that a person who did not hear in a fire was a person who had already decided where to go. And Eden looked at the north lane and began to walk toward it.
