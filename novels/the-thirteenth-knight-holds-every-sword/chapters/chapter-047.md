# Chapter 47: Something Said My Name Back

The corridor to the barracks was forty paces long with a wall on one side and the burning yard on the other. It had two doors and a turn in the middle, which meant a man could hold it if he had enough hands and not enough time to be afraid. The walls were stone on one side and boarded plaster on the other, and the plaster had begun to fall off in strips where the heat from the yard had got into it. A bucket of water stood by the near door with a rag in it, and a coat hung on a peg by the far one, which was the coat of a man who had gone through and had not come back for it. Eden had put his hand on both doors before choosing where to stand, and he had noted that the far door stuck in its frame and took half a second longer to open.

Lysette had taken the children through the far door and gone with them to the gate, leaving a strip of red cloth tied to the handle so that anyone following could see the way had been used. She had written three words on the back of a warrant before she left: CORRIDOR, FOUR, HOLD. Eden had read them while the fire came closer and had put the paper in his coat without saying that the number four included him.

Eden had four people and one corridor. Clara was on the stones with Rowan beside her, and the grey at the edges of her wound had spread two inches up her forearm where she had taken a Husk blade meant for his back. She had closed the wound with what she had left, and the edges had gone quiet, but the skin around them kept the colour of ash and did not go away. She had put her own glove over the line to hide it from Rowan and then taken it off again because he had already seen, and there was no point hiding a thing a person had watched arrive. Felix had looked at the grey once and had said nothing, and Clara's mouth had loosened when he did it.

"How long since you closed it?" Rowan asked.

"Four minutes."

"Does it hurt?"

"No." Clara looked down at the grey line. "That is the part I do not like."

"Sit still," Rowan said.

"I am sitting still."

"You are bleeding again."

"I am not." Clara pushed his hand away from the bandage. "Hold the corridor."

The turn was a place a man could stand because the wall made a corner and a body could not come round it in a straight line. Rowan had found that out on the first Husk and had stayed there, with his spear pointed at the corner and his feet set one behind the other, and he had not moved since.

Rowan held the corridor. His spear went into the first grey body that came round the turn and came out with the brass of its joint on the point. He planted his foot and brought the butt down on the next one. The corpses piled at the turn. The Husks had to climb over their own kind to reach him. He did not use the gold light again. There was no clean burst left in him after the yard, and his hands shook when he gripped the spear.

Eden was at the near door with **[Exa]** folded to the shield, taking the ones that came through the yard wall. Every strike rang on the iron ring and went into the stone, and the cold was in his arm up to the shoulder. He counted strikes because counting was a thing he could do while his body reported the hits.

Felix was at the far door with the bow held low and no arrow on the string. He had shot his last clean shot at the coal pile and he had refused the second because **[Osk]** was one arrow and total quiet, and neither existed in a corridor full of fire. He stood with his back to the door and watched the dark behind him, because the dark behind him was where the children had gone.

Eden could not be at both doors. He looked at Felix, and then at the space between them, and he made three knocks on the stone with the iron ring.

The knocks went through the corridor and through the smoke, and Felix turned because three knocks was not a sound anyone made by accident in a fight. He put his hand out behind him and said, in a clear voice, "Perrin."

The shield left Eden's arm and was in Felix's hand in the time it took a man to turn. **[Thessa]** was a round shield with a plain iron face and a strap worn smooth by someone else's hand. It came when it was called the way a dog comes to a whistle. It had always come back to its owner since the day it was made. It had come to Felix now because Eden had lent it, and the loan had been answered with a name. Felix took it by the strap and held it low. The weight settled on his front foot and did not shift, even when the door frame shook from a blow on the other side. He looked at the face of it, then at his own boots in the snow, and then at the near door where Eden stood, and he said nothing until the first blow arrived.

Felix looked at the mark inside the strap: a small raised feather with one bar bent out of line. He put his thumb on it and kept it there while a Husk came through the far door and struck the shield. The blow rang once and went nowhere, and the shield stayed where his hand had put it.

Felix stood with the shield out and his eyes on the far door. "Wren."

"Yes."

"Three knocks and a name. That is a loan, isn't it?"

"Yes."

"Can I give it back?"

"Not while you are holding it."

"I did not think so." Felix turned the shield a little so the iron face caught the firelight. "I have been trying to work out what you do at night for five years, and you answer me with a shield that comes when I say my middle name."

"It was the only answer that fitted."

"It fits too well." He put his thumb back on the feather mark. "Something said my name back," he said, in the small flat voice he used for things he had decided not to understand.

"That was the shield," Eden said.

"I know what it was." Felix turned the shield a little and the feather mark went out of sight. "I did not know a thing could answer a name."

Rowan called from the turn. "We are losing the corridor."

Eden went back to the near door. He took two Husks on the shield and put the butt of **[Exa]** into a grey throat on the third, and the third one went down across the threshold where the others had to step over it. Fire moved closer to the turn behind them. A burning beam fell across the corridor and split it in two. Sparks went up and died in the snow.

Clara moved to follow Rowan as he shifted position. She had been sitting with her hand on the stone, and when she stood the wound at her forearm opened again and the grey at its edges spread another inch. Rowan turned and caught her by the elbow before she fell, and he put himself between her and the turn with the spear across his body, and he held there with his back to the enemy for four heartbeats while she sat down again on the stone.

"I can close it," she said.

"Do it."

"It will cost what is left."

"Then take what is left." Rowan's voice shook once and then went steady. "I can hold for a minute. I cannot hold for a minute and watch you die as well."

Clara pressed her hand to the wound. The grey edges stopped moving and the skin closed from the sides in, and when she took her hand away there was a dark line where the grey had been, like a pencil mark that would not rub out. She sat with her back against the wall and her eyes open and her hands in her lap, and Rowan stood in front of her with the spear point at the turn. He put his hand behind him without looking. She put her hand into it. He held it for a moment and then let go, because he needed both hands on the spear.

"I am all right," she said.

"I know."

"No, you do not." Clara kept her eyes open. "Say it properly."

"You are all right." Rowan's voice broke on the last word and he set his feet again. "I can hold for a minute."

"Then hold it."

Eden looked once and then went back to his door, because a corridor did not hold itself while a person watched someone they loved.

The sounds changed at the far end. Felix's shield rang once, twice, and then a third time, and the third strike drove him back two steps into the door frame. He held. The shield came back to his arm after the blow as if it had never left it, and he shifted his feet and put it into the next grey reach.

"Wren," Felix said. "I do not know how much longer I can hold this."

"Hold until the children are through."

"They are through." Felix's voice went flat. "They have been through for six minutes."

"Then hold until I say stop."

"Tell me what to do." Felix did not turn round. "Do not tell me why. Do not tell me it will be all right. Just tell me what to do."

Eden put the ring against the near door and heard the strike coming before it arrived. He had two choices and one of them was a word. "Hold the door," he said, with the ring already against the stone. "Count to twenty out loud so I can hear it through the smoke. If I have not come back, take the shield and go through the far door with it."

"Twenty out loud," Felix said. He repeated the number once, which was something he did when he wanted a number to stay where he had put it. "One. Two."

The corridor went on burning, and the shield rang under his hand at three, and he kept counting out loud into the smoke.
