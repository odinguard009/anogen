# Chapter 47: The Round Shield

The boy with the burning sleeve had run north. The smoke had taken him. And Clara had gone after him with her staff in her hand. And Eden had followed the sound of her boots until the corridor turned. The sound was lost. Lysette was behind him with her list. And Felix was at the corner with his bow. The four of them stood for a moment with the fire going on around them. No clear word among them.

The corridor to the barracks was narrow. The smoke came along it in low layers. And Eden went first with his hand on the wall. His shield across his chest. And Felix followed with his bow in one hand. His hatchet in the other. And Lysette came behind with her batons out. Her book pushed into her belt.

"They cannot both be held," Lysette said. She meant the corridor and the north lane. And Eden knew it, and he did not answer, because there was one of him and two places to be, and that was the whole of the problem.

Lysette read the names on her sheet in a low voice as they went. She stopped at the name of the conscript boy. Said that the mark beside it was still empty. And Felix said that an empty mark was not the same as a dead mark. She said that she knew. That she had written both kinds before. That she preferred the empty ones. Eden heard her and said nothing. Because the corridor ahead had gone dark in a way smoke alone did not make dark.

A Husk came out of the smoke ahead of them with its hands up. And Felix put an arrow to his string and stopped. Because the thing was between them and the door they wanted. There was no room in the corridor to shoot past it. Eden moved to knock on the hard shadow behind it. Then stopped, because the smoke in the corridor had no edge that he could trust,. A opening needed an edge or it would not open.

Felix put the bow down and took something from his coat. It was round and iron-faced and worn at the strap. It was lighter than it looked. There was a small mark under the strap that caught the light from the fire.

"Three knocks," Eden said, and his voice went flat and quiet. "Knock on the ring three times and answer when it asks."

"Asks what?"

"Your name. The one your mother gave you."

Felix looked at the shield. Then at Eden, and then at the Husk coming down the corridor, and he knocked three times on the ring with his knuckles, and something said a name back out of the iron, and it was not a voice and was not not a voice, and it said "Perrin."

Felix's face changed. "Something said my name back."

"Answer it."

"Perrin," Felix said, and he said it clearly, and the shield came awake in his hand with a low sound like a door turning on a hinge, and he put his arm through the strap and the iron settled onto his forearm as if it had been measured for him.

"**[Thessa]**," Eden said, and the shield was round and iron-faced with a worn strap, and it covered chin to knee when held low, and it came back to the arm that held it if a hand let go, and there was a small feather mark under the strap that Eden had seen once before in a dream.

"Why does it have a feather? Felix asked, and he did not look away from the Husk.

"I will tell you later."

"Don't tell me. Just tell me what to do."

"Put it between the door and the Husk and walk."

Felix walked, and the shield took the first blow with a sound that went down the corridor, and the Husk's long arm stopped against the iron and it had hit a wall, and Felix pushed with his shoulder and the shield went forward, and the Husk went back into the smoke, and Lysette put two baton strokes into the joint of its knee and it went down.

The round shield took the second blow from the Husk and held. And Felix pushed the thing back into the wall. Kept it there with the iron edge under its throat. And Lysette came past him with her batons. Struck the joint at its elbow. The arm fell, and the Husk folded down onto the stone. Felix stood with his breath hard in his chest. The shield still on his arm. He said that the shield was warm. And Eden said that it always was. And Felix said that he had not known shields could be warm.

A sound came from behind them. And Clara was in the doorway of the barracks with a conscript boy held against her side. The boy's coat was no longer burning. Her left arm hung oddly. The edge of a wound at her shoulder had a grey line running into it where Husk blood had touched the skin. She came three steps and then stopped. Her face was very white, and she kept the boy behind her.

"Close it," Eden said, and he meant the wound.

"I can close it," Clara said. She put her staff against the wall. Took her hand off the boy. She closed her eyes, and the grey at the edge of the wound stopped moving and then went dark and then was gone, and she opened her eyes, and her mouth was tight with the cost of it, and she sat down on the step because her legs would not hold her.

Rowan came down the corridor with his hands out. His face open with worry. He got to his knees beside her. Took the hand that was not hurt. He said her name three times because he did not know what else to say. She said that she was all right. He said that she was not. She said that she was all right enough. He said that there was a difference. He was going to sit there until she was all and all right. She shut her eyes and let him hold her hand.

Felix stood with the round shield on his arm. The feather mark under the strap. He looked at the shield. Then at Eden, and then at the smoke at the far end of the corridor, and he said, "I am going to put this down in a minute, and when I do, I want you to tell me what it is, and I want the whole answer this time."

"Yes," Eden said.

"Not the answer you give Rowan. The real one."

"Yes," Eden said, and he said it quietly, and he put his hand on the wall beside the boy who had been saved, and the boy put his hand on Eden's sleeve and would not let go, and Eden stood still with the shield warm against his arm and the corridor full of smoke and the sound of Rowan talking to Clara in a low voice that did not stop.

Lysette knelt beside them with her book shut and her hands folded. She looked at the round shield. The feather mark and then at EdenShe wrote nothing down. After a while she got up. Went to the door to watch the north lane. She stood there with her back to them. Her shoulders were straight.

They sat in the corridor for a while because nobody was ready to stand up. The smoke thinned and the cold came back through the door. The boy sat with his back against the wall. His hands in his lap. Watched Felix and Felix were a thing he had never seen before. Felix let him watch, and he kept the shield on his arm, and after a while he took it off and put it across his knees, and the feather mark showed on the inside where the strap had rubbed it bright.

"Who is Perrin? the boy asked.

"Me," Felix said.

"Is that your name?"

"It is the one my mother gave me. It is the one the shield knows. And I am beginning to think those are not the same as the name on the roll. Felix looked at Eden. "I want the whole answer now."

Eden told him about the forge. The door and the man who had carried a sword of gold light. He told it plainly, and he left out the parts that were not his to give, and Felix listened with the shield across his knees and his face closed, and when Eden was finished Felix said that it was not the whole answer, and Eden said that it was the whole one he had, and Felix said that he had known that would be the answer and had wanted to hear it said out loud, and then he put the shield back on his arm and said that he would keep it until Eden asked for it back, and that he would not ask again that night.

Lysette came in from the door. Said that the north lane was quiet. That the cook had counted forty people out of the south gate. That the lists matched. She looked at the round shield. At Felix and then at EdenShe said that there was a great deal she was not writing down. That she was going to write it all down in the morning. She walked away down the corridor with her book under her arm. Her shoulders straight. And Eden watched her go. Then looked at the wall because it was easier than looking at the door where she had been.

Clara slept against the wall with Rowan's coat over her. Rowan sitting awake beside her with his hands wrapped around his own. The boy slept with his head on Felix's shoulder. And Felix sat very still so as not to wake him. The shield lay across his knees with the feather mark turned up. And Eden sat with his back to the door. Listened to the camp settling after the fire. He did not sleep at all.

The boy slept for a while with his head on Felix's shoulder. Then he woke and asked whether the fire was out. And Felix said that it was. The boy asked whether his coat was still burning. And Felix said that it was not. The boy put a hand on his own sleeve. Found only cloth. He said that it was the first good thing that had happened to him that day. And Clara laughed once from the wall. The laugh turned into a soft sound because her shoulder hurt. And Rowan put his hand on her forehead and told her not to laugh, and she told him to stop fussing, and he said that he would fuss if he wanted to, and she shut her eyes and did not tell him to stop.

Lysette came back down the corridor. Stood over the list with her pencil out. And she drew a mark beside the conscript boy's name. And she wrote a small number after it. And Felix asked what the number meant. And she said that it was the count of people she had not written off. And that the boy was in it. And that she had put him at the top because he was the smallest. And Felix said that was a good place for him. And she said that it was not a good place. It was a true one, and she put the pencil behind her ear and went back to the door.

Eden watched the mark on the sheet. Then the boy sleeping with his hand on Felix's sleeve. And he thought about a round shield that came back to the arm that held it. And about a name spoken out of iron. And about a feather mark that a boy with a bow had seen. Understood without asking anyone to explain it. And he put his own glove back on. Fastened the cuff twice. And he sat with his back to the door. Waited for the morning.
