# Chapter 48: The Grafted Crown

The camp had not slept. The north gate was a line of men with soot on their faces. The kitchen fire had gone out under a pot of water. The east gate was the only part of the camp that looked like a school in session. Halvard Strent stood there with his coat buttoned and his hair flat. Two masters were carrying a table out of the map hut because he had asked for oneA third was bringing a chair. Nobody asked why the table was needed in the middle of a burned yard.

Halvard Strent arrived at the east gate before the sun was up with a clipboard under his arm. A packet of orders in his hand. He began to organise the teachers and the camp were a late examination. The fire were a minor noise in the corridor. He sent two masters to the infirmary. One to the round sheet and one to the gate. He spoke to each of them in the same calm voice. By the time Eden came past he had three lines of people moving in three directions. A fourth line waiting for a number.

Eden stopped and looked at him. And Strent looked back over his glasses.

"Wren," he said. "You are covered in soot. Go and wash."

"Where did you come from?"

"From the east road. The school head sent me when the wire came. Strent wrote a line on his sheet. "There is a Crown in the tents. His name is Desmond HarrowHe is of GallowmarchHis graft is old. Badly cared for. He said it as if he were reading the weather. "Do not let him near the boys."

Rowan came across the yard with his coat over one arm. His hands still grey from the smoke. He said that Clara was sleeping in the infirmary. That the boy with the burnt sleeve was beside her. Would not let go of her glove. Strent wrote both names on his sheet. Asked whether either of them needed a master. And Rowan said that they needed tea. That a master would only write something down. And Strent said that tea was already being sent. And Rowan looked at him for a moment. Then said thank you. Because it was true.

A tent collapsed at the far end of the camp while they spoke. The canvas came down in a long grey fold. Its shadow ran across the ground like a dark river. And Eden saw the shape of the man inside it before he saw the man himself.

The Crown of Gallowmarch stepped out of the fallen canvas with his right arm held low. The sleeve of his coat had split from shoulder to cuff. The arm beneath it was grey from the shoulder down. The grey had climbed his neck on one side. Reached the corner of his eye. He was not a large man. His face was calm in and a face is calm when the person in it has decided everything already. He walked toward the nearest group of conscripts without hurry.

"**[Grask]**," he said, and the word came out of him with the grey.

The **[Grask]** was his grafted arm swollen grey until the hand spanned a man's chest. The sleeve split at the seam. It crushed without a blade. Each use sent the grey higher up his neck. He did not seem to care. He caught a teacher by the coat. Lifted him off the ground with one hand. The teacher's boots kicked in the air. And Strent called the man's name. Told him to put the teacher down. Valerius was already moving, and he was not running, and the men who had been holding the line made room because his sword was out and his face said that the room was too small. Eden went the other way with the shield on his arm. He passed behind the fallen canvas where the shadow lay in a long fold. He knocked twice on the edge of it. Went into the dark with the lance across his chest. He came out beside a water barrel where no one was standingA Husk that had been coming at the teachers went past him into the empty space. Was gone.

Harrow turned his head and looked at Strent with interest. and a man who gave orders in that voice were a thing worth a second glance.

Eden went through the fire with his shield over his head. His hood pulled tight. The heat took the breath out of his mouth. He came around the side of a burning tent. Put his hand on the light edge where the falling canvas threw its shadow across the stone. He tapped twice, and the dark opened under the fold, and the shadow went into it, and the canvas followed the shadow because there was nothing else for it to do, and a line of Husks that had been coming around the tent ran into a space where the tent had been and stopped.

Eden came out behind Harrow with the lance low. He struck the grey arm at the elbow where the sleeve had split. The grey was thin. The arm dropped the teacher. Who fell to his knees. Crawled away with his hands over his head. Harrow turned with his grey arm coming up again. His face was calm and his eye was bright. He said something in a language Eden did not know. Then said. In plain words, "The bottom of the world smells like this."

Eden knew the smell: wet iron and old bread and hot grey dust. It had come up through the tunnels under the camp. It was in the man's coat and in his breath. He stepped back because the grey arm could reach farther than it looked.

Valerius came through the smoke with his sword in his hand. His white band dark with soot. He put himself between Harrow and the teachers. The air near his shoulder frosted for one breath and cleared. And Harrow laughed and swung the grey arm at him. And Valerius took it on his blade. Slid sideways with his feet well placed. The two of them moved together in a small circle with the fire behind them. The teachers getting out of and.

Eden went around the circle with the shield up. Looking for the one inch of the graft that the gold could show. He found it at the wrist where the grey met the skin in a line as thin as a hair. He had half a step to take when Harrow turned. Saw the conscript boy standing alone by the water barrel with his hands full of tin cups.

The grey arm went past Valerius's blade and across the space. And Harrow caught the boy with his left hand. Put a knife at his throat in one motion. He held the boy in front of him with his grey arm hanging at his side. The knife was small and clean and very steady.

The teachers stopped, and Valerius stopped, and the smoke went over all of them, and the boy's eyes were wide above the blade, and he did not cry out because his throat would not let him.

Harrow looked at Eden and smiled with the corner of his mouth that the grey had not reached.

"Well," he said. "Which of you is the one I came for?"

The smoke thinned for a moment. The yard showed itself in patches: the table where Strent was writing. The line of teachers moving the wounded. The black square where the tent had been. And Valerius with his sword up. His feet planted in the ash. Lysette came out of the infirmary with her book under her arm. Her batons in her hand. She stopped when she saw the knife at the boy's throat. And she did not call out. Because a shout was a thing a man with a knife could hear. Act on. And she put one hand on the wall and stood very still.

Felix came behind her with the round shield on his arm. And he did not have his bow. And he put the shield across his chest. Said in a low voice that he was not going to shoot. And Lysette said that she knew. And he said that he had one arrow left. That it was in his coat. That he was not a fool. And she said that she knew that as well. And he took his place beside her without another word. And the two of them stood with their backs to the wall. Their faces toward the smoke.

The boy made a small sound. Harrow moved the knife a hair. And the boy stopped, and Valerius said the man's name and then said nothing, because a wrong word could take the boy's life, and Strent wrote a line on his sheet and put the sheet down on the table and walked away from it with his hands empty, which was the first uncontrolled thing he had done all morning.

Eden stood with the shield low and his hand on the lance. And he looked at the grey arm. At the knife and at the boy's face. And he knew that there was one way to end it. That and would show him to every person in the yard. And he did not move. Because the boy was breathing in short small pulls. The knife was very steady. The morning was not the time to be seen.

Harrow waited, and then he said the name again, and this time he said it toward the teachers and not to Eden: "Which of you is the one I came for? and the teachers looked at one another. And Lysette's hand closed over the round sheet. And Valerius's mouth tightened, and the boy's eyes went to Eden and then away, and the smoke came between them and the moment broke into pieces.

Felix came up beside Eden with his hatchet in his hand. The round shield still on his arm. And he said that the boy at the water barrel was the one who had carried the pots in the kitchen. And that he was eleven. And that his name was on the round sheet with no mark beside it. And Eden said that he knew. Felix said that a person who knew a thing like that. Did nothing about it was a person he did not understand. And Eden said that he was going to do something about it. And Felix said that he had heard that before. That it was time for the doing. Not the saying, and he put the shield up and took his place beside Valerius.

Lysette stood at the edge of the smoke with her batons down. Her book shut. And she counted the teachers moving out of the yard. Called the numbers to Strent. Who wrote them on his sheet. And when she had finished she said that there was one teacher still inside the infirmary and that she was going back for him. And Strent said that he would go, and she said that he would be slower, and he said that this was true but that a vice-chair did not go into a fire alone, and she looked at him for a moment and then went with him, and the two of them disappeared into the smoke with their heads down.

Harrow held the boy with one hand. The knife with the other. And the grey at his eye had reached the lid. And his face was calm and patient. And he said that he had come a long way for a name and that he would wait a little longer. And nobody answered him, and the smoke went over the yard and the fire cracked in the tents behind him, and the boy's hands stayed still at his sides because he had been told that moving made a knife cut, and he was a boy who did what he was told.
