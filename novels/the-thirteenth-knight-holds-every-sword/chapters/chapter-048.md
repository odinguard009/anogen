# Chapter 48: Harrow

Harrow was dying. He was an old soldier. He had been on the Wall for twenty years. He had fought the Hush. He had fought the Pale. Now he was dying.

Eden sat beside him. Harrow was in bed. His face was grey. His hands were cold. His breath was shallow.

"Eden," Harrow said. "You are here."

"Yes."

"I am dying."

"I know."

Harrow looked at him. His eyes were clear. "I have a message. For you. From the Thirteenth Vigil."

Eden leaned forward. "What message?"

Harrow coughed. Blood. "She stopped reading me," he said.

Eden did not understand. "Who stopped reading you?"

"Aiwa," Harrow said. "The planet. She stopped reading me. I am dying. She does not read the dead."

Eden's heart stopped. "Aiwa. You know about Aiwa?"

"I know everything," Harrow said. "I was Thirteenth Vigil. Before you. I was the last one. Before you."

Eden stared at him. "You were Thirteenth Vigil?"

"Yes. I hunted Veils. I protected the Wall. I was alone. Like you."

Harrow coughed again. More blood. "I have something for you." He reached under his pillow. He pulled out a small stone. It was dark. It glowed faintly.

"This is a reading stone," Harrow said. "It shows you what Aiwa sees. What she reads. Hold it. You will see."

Eden took the stone. It was warm. It glowed. He held it. He felt something. A presence. Vast. Old. Alive.

Aiwa. The planet. The mind. She was there. In the stone. In the Wall. In everything.

"She is real," Eden whispered.

"She is real," Harrow said. "She reads us. She names us. She is the mother of all."

Harrow's eyes closed. His breath stopped. He was dead.

Eden sat beside him. He held the reading stone. He felt Aiwa. She was vast. She was old. She was sad.

She had stopped reading Harrow. Because Harrow was dead. She did not read the dead.

Eden looked at the stone. He would keep it. He would use it. He would learn what Aiwa saw.

He put the stone in his pocket. He closed Harrow's eyes. He stood.

He left the room. He went to the roof. He looked at the sky. The stars were out. The Pale was dark. The Wall held.

He thought about Aiwa. The planet. The mother. She read everyone. She named everyone. She was the registry. The Bell. The voice that said "Black Lancer" and "White Swordsman."

She was real. She was alive. And she was sad.

Eden would find out why. He always found out.
