## Ch 13 - The Eleventh Lamp
At night on the Long Stair Eden sees the lamplighter snuffing lamps instead of lighting them, shadow not breathing; the Veil wearing Lothar Pell flings a pole-cup of burning oil, which soaks Eden's left glove, and Eden drops it through a door in the step and kills it with Ea, leaving boots, coat, pole and grey dust; his palm is burned. At dawn a Year One girl reports a murder; Lysette drafts Eden to hold the cordon rope and notes his new glove. Golden Seal Inspector Rochefort claims the scene, Lysette invokes the charter's one hour. Her Witness shows a figure in black and a tall door with a ring; Okoru identifies Veil dust and tells the inspector to inform Pell's wife; Lysette lays the Testimony on a glass plate, and Eden watches how good she is. Her vow: "The Black Order was here. I'll find out who opens the doors."

## Ch 14 - Seven for Seven
Epigraph: "An absence is a debt. Debts are collected." One scene on the roof: Eden attempts a perfect seven-day nap record (a bet with Felix) against Lysette's week of warrants, runners and ropes, while Felix, a self-declared double agent paid in lunch, reports from the apple tree (Seraphine's joke 19, a baker pun). Eden deduces that no runner has climbed the roof stair because Lysette's own notice, "closed for repair", has guarded it all week. She finally comes up, finds him asleep (feigning) next to the rosemary label he once mended, realizes he has known the garden is hers for weeks, says nothing, covers him with her coat, waters the beds, tears the day's warrant into four pieces and plants them in the soil, then sits with her back to him.

## Ch 15 - The Hanging Loaf
Heading for the tannery Ember cell, Eden finds Lysette tailing him on a Committee pretext (she notes his loose shoulders "like carrying a tray"). He cannot lead her to the Embers, so he offers a harmless tour ending at Candlewick Lane: a refugee bakery with a "hanging loaf" ribbon rule, where children (Bodil, Halla, Idris) greet him, call Lysette "the thistle", and Mistress Brisa reveals he pays four silver weekly and talks about his "supervisor" constantly. Lysette watches him pay for bread he doesn't eat, writes "Unlicensed charity. No further action," and stays behind to "finish the form". That night, mid-complaint, the Dragon goes quiet and says "I promised someone"; he will not say who.
