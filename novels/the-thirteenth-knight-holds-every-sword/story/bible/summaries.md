# Chapter summaries (new draft; one entry per chapter as it is written)

(Chapters 1-10: see story/bible/archive/summaries-ch001-ch010.md)

## Ch 11 - The Direction of Shadows

In the first graded field run, Okoru sends the five Lances through the Undercroft maze with lamps, false walls and two timed constructs. Lance Nine works as a unit for the first time: Felix's Trail reads dragged stone, Lysette's Witness replays the hands that moved a wall, Rowan and Clara handle the constructs, and Eden reads the lamp shadows to find a gap over the exit without taking credit. They finish third in eleven minutes. A man in a staff coat watches Rowan from the dark gallery and vanishes when Eden looks up.



## Ch 12 - Perrin

Felix finds Eden behind the stables and asks why nobody gets close to him. Eden says he does not know how. Felix gives his middle name, Perrin, before asking for anything, and Eden says honestly that he has no name he can give back. Lysette arrives with a second lunch, says she is not losing track of him, and does not look at him once. Felix tells her his middle name on his way out. The two-lunch routine begins.



## Ch 13 - The Empty Frame

Eden ends a Veil wearing the Long Stair's lamplighter in the lamp-house at third bell and leaves no trace. In the morning Lysette runs the Committee inquiry while Eden stands against the wall. Her **[Witness]** shows a figure in black and a door-shaped shadow, and her **[Testimony]** shows the replay to the students before her wrist gives out. She finds an empty pale frame-shape on the wall, like one on her desk at school, and refuses to explain it. Last line: "The Thirteenth Vigil was here. I will find out who opens the doors."



## Ch 14 - The Herb Bed

Eden tries to keep a perfect nap record while Lysette's attendance campaign grows into warrants, runners and a stakeout. Felix plays double agent for lunch. Seraphine's joke count on Felix reaches nineteen. Eden sleeps in Lysette's herb bed for eleven minutes; she finds him, leaves him asleep, covers his boots with her coat, and asks him to tell Felix only the number. She knows he has kept her garden secret for a month.



## Ch 15 - The Boy Who Pays for Bread

Lysette follows Eden into the lower city on a Committee pretext while he scouts the dye-works from a distance for Ysolde. He knows she is there, changes route to keep her away from the Ember watcher, and ends at a refugee bakery where the children know his name. He pays for bread and does not eat it. The two agree not to write down the walk back. That night the Dragon stops mid-complaint with "I promised someone" and will not say who.





## Ch 16 - This Line Is Not Crossed

Valerius demands a sanctioned Ladder duel. In the sand yard he makes pale walls with ****[Injunction]****, each ruling costing a breath, while Eden stays behind them and refuses to use the lance point. Eden's ****[Hinge]**** turns a charge past him and lands the butt behind the knee; he keeps every miss within a finger's width. Valerius notices the restraint and the count of his breaths. The bell ends the round and Valerius drops his sword: "Again. And this time try."


## Ch 17 - A Finger's Width
The duel resumes. Valerius presses honestly and Eden repeats **[Hinge]** until it is no longer a surprise. He waits for the bell, moves his shield hand a finger's width, and lets the prince's blade touch him as the bell's sound begins, so the judges record a narrow loss. Lysette binds his split knuckles too hard and scolds while he lets her. Valerius, angry but controlled, says his father knows who Eden is, and so does he, and next time he will make him show them.


## Ch 18 - Alive, on the Night of Lights
Ysolde orders Eden to watch Rowan without being seen. Rowan notices everything except the grey-coat man following him through the lower city. In an unlit lane Eden opens a Door in a wall shadow, puts the watcher asleep in an empty cellar without a chase, and reads his book: Corwen, bagman. A folded copy of the exchange call list carries two added words, "Alive. Night of lights." Ysolde confirms the list is the Ball guest list. The Ball is nine days away.


## Ch 19 - The East Kitchen
Rowan volunteers Lance Nine for the cooking club's Ball-week bake. Clara heals a burn but cannot raise Seraphine's fallen soufflé, and Seraphine's joke count on Felix reaches forty-one and fails. When the oven throws flame across the kitchen, Eden silently folds **[Exa]** into the door-shield and turns the blast back into the oven; Seraphine calls it luck and he agrees. Rowan almost notices the garden soil on Lysette's apron but a dropped tray of rolls distracts him. Seraphine asks Lysette who she is going to the Ball with, and she writes "Nobody."


## Ch 20 - The Bend in the Line
At the war council Valerius hears ministers whisper of a hidden "Void Host" and orders a paper with names, not a summary. He studies the unexplained bend in the front around Verandel and sees his father's tiredness in the empty chair and the untouched cup. Count Merrow, his old map teacher, tells him to worry about the Ball instead: four hundred people, a hundred lanterns, one band, and the prince's name on the paper if anything goes wrong. Merrow asks which hall the lanterns hang in, and Valerius answers without thinking twice.

## Ch 21 - The Locket
Seraphine invites Eden to the Ball as a joke and he refuses quickly. Lysette writes her up for a crooked collar and cannot say why. On the roof Lysette admits she has no partner and takes out her silver locket, shut, worn once a year; she closes her hand over it when the Black Thirteen is mentioned. She tells Eden that her brother came home from court and could not dance, and that her mother wrote that House Hollen would act as if it were not. Eden says nothing useful and stays until she is done. She decides to go to the Ball "to prove I don't care."

## Ch 22 - The Smell of the Dust
Eden and Vesk enter the lower-city dye-works. Vesk uses **[Mott]** to wear a dead Ember's shadow and sends the four Cinder children out on a false drill order. Eden ends the three cell leaders one at a time with **[Draught]**, then wipes the room. A dying Ember's grey veins and the dry smell of the dust tell Eden that the cell is tied to the Veil network, though he does not know what the veins mean. The last Ember laughs: "You're late. The Crown already has the hall."

## Ch 23 - Thirteen Voices
Eden raids the second Ember safehouse in full **[Umra]**. Gorrath's **[Knock and Answer]** fills the street with thirteen voices from thirteen shadows, and the hired men inside surrender to an army that is not there. Eden finds the Ball plan: Rowan and Seraphine are named as guests to collect, but the written night is Friday, a day before the Ball. He takes the paper and a brass crown weight. Unlacing his left gauntlet, he finds a tiny feather stamped inside. Ysolde then doubles the Justiciar Seal guard and orders him away from the Ball.

## Ch 24 - The Eleventh Bell
Eden watches the Ball from the roof above the glass atrium while Lysette, in a gown, cries over Rowan dancing with Clara. At the eleventh bell the lanterns go out and the chandelier falls; Eden catches it in a Door cut from its shadow across the roof glass, unseen by the room. The Ashen Crowns then seize the atrium with blades at throats and demand two guests. An Ember points up through the glass and shouts for "the Thornmere boy, Wren"; Lysette hears him say they thought he was theirs.

## Ch 25 - The Lattice
Lysette fetches the guard while Eden re-enters the seized atrium through Doors cut in the roof lattice's shadows. Ilsabet Rorke wears a graft in her right arm and uses **[Hrak]**; Eden takes a blade on his shield and answers with the first **[Lintel]**, a flat overhead shield with **[Inead]** drawn under it in one motion. The hostages see only a dark shape and a gold glint. He spares Ilsabet and uses the lattice shadows to move the students out. Valerius sees more than he should; Merrow admires an Ostrevaal wall hanging. At the door Lysette says she saw smoke where a man should have been and orders Eden back to the roof before heads are counted.

## Ch 26 - On My Foot
Back on the roof after the Ball siege, Lysette asks Eden whether he was up there the whole time and he answers "No." She accepts it. They stand on each other's feet for a slow dance while the band still plays below; he smiles once, a real one, and she does not mention it. Eden sleeps without the war dream for the first time in years. At dawn he finds his gloves on the stone with the handkerchief inside, washed and folded with the feather turned inward.

## Ch 27 - The Year on the Page
The steam press prints a paper blaming the Vigil for lawlessness at the Ball. Lysette folds it away when she reaches a Seal notice about the Hollen affair of PR 110; Eden reads the year twice and records a cold stomach and a pulse count without saying anything. Finals keep him at eight, and the board gives his first on-page reading, **[Knight]** and **[Black Lancer]**. Valerius walks past without a word. On the roof Lysette opens the locket to show him a young man's face, and Eden looks at the apple tree for eleven seconds. A Seal inquiry into the Ball night's unknown knight is posted.

## Ch 28 - The House at Seven
Eden and Felix take the veinline train to Silverfell, where the Argentine household is loud and the Duke pulls them both into dinner. Bandits on the carriage road are dealt with over soup and fish: a pistol misfires twice near Bertram and he takes it away politely, with no one hurt. Bertram's boots were muddy at dawn; the Duke nearly says "your mother had w-" before Greta's ladle strikes a dish; and a bell rings once by itself when the Duke laughs. Felix asks which of the family's noise and Eden's quiet is the act.

## Ch 29 - The Crown with No Centre
Felix presses Eden for the truth about his quietness and gets a half-truth he can live with. On the lake the Duke tries again to speak about Liora and something she was making for Eden, but cannot finish; they fish in silence and he puts his one catch back. That night the Tutor sets a board with nine pawns and tells Eden that a king once failed his test. On the edge of the board sits a crown with no centre.
