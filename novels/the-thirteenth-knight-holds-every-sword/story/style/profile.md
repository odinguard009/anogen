# Style profile (swappable; engine reads only this file for style). Rewritten whole 2026-11-26 (owner orders 2026-11-25 and 2026-11-26 merged; no older rule is left standing against a newer one).

## 1. LEVEL: simple English for learners (permanent; wins every conflict)
Write for readers who recently learned English (about CEFR A2-B1). The story stays exciting; only the language is simple.
- Most common everyday words (about the 2,000-3,000 most common). Replace rare, formal, literary or old words ("stairs" not "flights"; "went quiet" not "fell silent"; "hide" not "conceal"). Avoid phrasal verbs when one plain verb works.
- Idioms: at most ONE easy one per chapter, only where the sentence makes the meaning clear, never inside fights, plans, key emotions or clues. No slang that needs culture ("flight risk", "break the ice").
- One idea per sentence. Mostly 6-14 words, mean 9-12; link ideas with and, but, because, so, then, when, after. No more than 3 short (under 6 words) sentences in a row except in a fast fight. Simple past, active voice. No semicolons, no parentheses, no sentence over 30 words, at most one which/that/who clause.
- Concrete nouns (door, rope, knife, bread). Numbers and distances said clearly (in steps).
- Figurative language: at most one simple comparison per page, taken from this world (lamps, ink, locks, bells, motor-cars), never a stock simile ("like a hen"). Jokes work from plain words.
- Keep paragraphs short (1-4 lines on a phone).

## 2. CLARITY AND PICTURE (permanent)
1. NAME BUDGET: at most 12 distinct capitalized names and terms per chapter (the checker fails above 16). Background people get no name ("the clerk"). At most 2 new named people per chapter, each with one plain tag (job or look). Bold-bracket labels (section 6) are registered in canon_names.txt and do not use this budget.
2. WORLD TERMS: at most 2 new per chapter. Plain thing first, then the name ("the black armor, Umra"). One thing, one name. Explain in the same or next sentence.
3. PLAIN NOUNS: swap or explain in 3-4 words (ledger = book; refectory = dining hall; registrar = office clerk).
4. PICTURE RULE: every scene opens with 2-3 short sentences: where, when, the light, one sound or smell, who is there. Each named person gets 2 visible traits (face, hair, clothes, size) and one way of moving or talking on first appearance. In long scenes, say again where people stand. Monsters get size, color and shape compared to a thing everyone knows.
5. FIGHT PICTURE: before action, say where each person stands, how far (in steps) and what is around. Then one clear move per sentence. In the dark, say how the hero knows (sound, touch, a match).
6. SAY THE PLAN: when Eden does something clever, one plain sentence says what he wants and why, before or right after, as observed action or a flat thought ("Eden needed light. Light makes edges. Edges make doors.").
7. Chapter length 1800-2500 words. Description replaces side details, never story events.

## 3. POV AND VOICE (owner order 2026-11-26: CLOSE THIRD PERSON; replaces first person everywhere)
- Close third person, past tense, one point of view per scene, no head-hopping inside a scene. The camera follows Eden from OUTSIDE: what he does, looks at, counts, says, and what others do and say about him.
- EDEN IS FLAT. The camera never enters his feelings: no "he felt", "he was sad", "his heart ached". Allowed: body data and counts ("His stomach was cold. Lunch was two hours ago."), small wrong actions when he is moved (he fixes the wrong thing, stops mid-sentence), plain thoughts about plans and facts, and other people's reactions to him. He reads other people's moods like dial readings and misreads his own. He is never the most emotional person in a scene. Cracks are rare and physical (the lullaby in ch 8). He snaps only when his people are hurt on purpose: he goes polite and very quiet and takes off his left glove.
- Other point-of-view characters (Lysette, Valerius, the Duke, Ysolde) may have inner feeling in their own scenes. That contrast is how the reader learns what Eden does not say.
- HUMOR lives in dialogue, objects and Eden's literal replies ("Eaten today?" "Technically."). No wry narrator commentary.
- VARYING SENTENCE OPENERS in third person: do not start more than two sentences in a row with Eden/He/She. Start with a place, an object, a sound, a time, a number, someone else's action or a line of speech ("The stairs were empty." "Three steps ahead, she stopped."). The checker warns when one word starts over 22% of the sentences.
- Eden: quiet engineer of outcomes; notices exits, shadows, who is lying, what it costs. Lazy on the surface (naps, skips, shrugs), prepared underneath (he scouted it three nights ago). Never boasts; never explains a plan before it works. Realist with a few hard rules (no kill without confirmation; no human kills with the Emperor's knights).
- THE VIGIL WORKS SILENT, SECRET, PRECISE: no chases through crowds, no shouting, no witnesses. The Thirteenth Vigil is a half-believed legend; ordinary people have never seen the black armor. A mission is one quiet room, one kill, a trace wiped.
- OPENINGS: one odd, specific concrete detail only this story has, then action; never weather or a tour of the setting. ENDINGS: stop on an action, an object or a line of dialogue; no summary sentence, no explained feeling, no moral.

## 4. DIALOGUE MODEL (owner order 2026-11-26; model scenes: archive/restart-2026-11-25/chapters/chapter-001.md, Eden and Lysette on the roof; chapter-002.md, Eden and Valerius at the stairs)
OWNER EXCEPTION: these two scenes are the only superseded text a writer may use. Take their structure, beats and named lines ("Did it light?", "Fifty-two minutes.", "No." / "Yes.", "Pity.") and rewrite them in close third person. Do not copy their wording otherwise: those drafts are first person and contain tells the checker bans ("ears went red", "Nobody", the wasp-nest simile). Same easy English; livelier and more impressive because of HOW lines are built. The owner's rule stands: dialogue is 25-40% of a chapter's words (the checker reports an issue below 25% and above the talking-heads limit). Never pad to reach it (rule 8).
1. EACH SPEAKER HAS A HABIT the reader hears without tags. Lysette: numbers, procedure, orders ("Fifty-two minutes." "Walk."). Eden: short, literal, accidentally funny ("The stone is loud."). Felix: a running joke ("Eaten today?" "Technically."). Valerius: full names, few words, each with weight ("Brandmeier. A ward with no subject." ... "Pity."). Every new character gets a habit in the first scene.
2. THE SCENE CIRCLES AN OBJECT, not a feeling: a handkerchief, a trowel, a burnt paper lantern, a timetable. People talk about the object because they cannot talk about the thing under it.
3. THE KIND LINE IS A SMALL ODD QUESTION ("Did it light?") that opens a person more than comfort does.
4. ECHO AND CALLBACK: a word returns changed ("Most." / "I was awake for the last four."). A short line answers a long one. People interrupt, dodge, answer another question, trail off.
5. BUILD A STRONG CHARACTER THROUGH OTHERS FIRST: the prince arrives through the empty space people leave around him, officers who leave at a nod, a bow, two people answering one question at once ("No." / "Yes."). Show the effect on the room before his line.
6. THE LAST LINE OWNS THE SCENE: end on the line that costs someone something ("Pity.") or on an object (a written question the other person half reads). No explaining after.
7. ACTION BEATS FROM OBJECTS instead of adverbs (the trowel point down along her arm). Tags are mostly "said".
8. NO FILLER DIALOGUE. Every exchange changes something: a fact, trust, power or a plan. Never add chatter (clerks, gardeners, small talk) to reach the percentage. If a chapter is short of dialogue, add a real exchange or accept a lower number and tell the critic. PENDING PROPOSAL TO THE OWNER (not applied; the 25-40% rule above is still in force): make the number a warning only.

## 5. HUMAN FEEL (the text must not read as AI-written)
- Small opinions come from characters, not from a narrator. Odd, specific details a person would notice (a chipped cup, a stale roll). Uneven rhythm: a one-line paragraph, then a longer sentence with and/but/because.
- Not allowed: a tidy moral or summary at the end of a paragraph or scene, "not X but Y" or "It wasn't X. It was Y." more than once per chapter, rule-of-three lists as a habit, stacked similes, repeated gestures (sigh, clenched fists, ears going red), explaining a feeling after showing it.
- Allowed: a flat joke, a wandering thought, a small mistake. Do not reuse a simile, gesture or sentence pattern more than once per arc. Each chapter has at least one surprising detail that belongs only to this world. End each chapter on a hook of a different kind from the previous ones.

## 6. NAME LABELS: **[Name]** (owner order 2026-11-26; permanent)
- Every NAMED weapon, ability, technique, signature or ultimate move, job and class is written in bold square brackets EVERY time it is named in narration or in an on-page reading: **[Inead]**, **[Exa]**, **[Lintel]**, **[Witness]**, **[Black Lancer]**, **[Unbind: Evrin]**. Nameless things stay plain ("a sword", "a spell").
- Class and job words are bracketed only when they mean the registered class or a reading: "his class, **[Black Lancer]**", "the reading said **[Knight]**". The ordinary noun stays plain: "a knight", "the Rampart Host's knights", "an archer".
- In spoken dialogue the name is plain ("Draw Inead."). PROPOSAL TO THE OWNER: confirm this, or ask for brackets in dialogue too.
- First use: the first-use description (look, body, effect, limit) in the SAME scene. Later uses: the label plus one fresh detail. The label never replaces description. At most 2 NEW labelled names per chapter; there is no cap on repeats or on how many known names appear in a big fight. Owners and names: power-system.md section 13; first uses: first-use.md.

## 7. FIRST USE OF A WEAPON, ARMOR OR ABILITY (permanent)
The first time a named character uses a weapon, armor, relic or ability on the page, describe it in 2-4 plain sentences INSIDE the action: (1) what it looks like, (2) how the body uses it, (3) what it does to the target or space, (4) its limit or cost. Later uses add only what is new. Check story/bible/first-use.md before writing a use; add the line after a first use.

## 8. CANON FOLLOWED EXACTLY
- Aura, mana, Tenet, class/job and spell words: story/bible/power-system.md (one word, one meaning; no ranks; standing of the circle: section 17). Tech: tech.md (late 1890s: motor-cars, telephones, trams, mana-lamps, steam presses; at most 2 new tech terms per chapter; a gun never decides a knight's fight). Dates: calendar.md (it wins). Reveals: reveal-ladder.md (hint, doubt, evidence, confirm, in order, with gaps; never early). Unbind never appears without earlier hints. Lysette's look: characters.md.

## 9. SCENE FEEL AND ARMOR (permanent)
- Pick each scene's mood in 2-3 words (tense, warm, sad, funny, cold, quiet-before-trouble) and let place, light, sound, sentence speed, verbs and hands carry it. One scene, one main mood; a change needs a clear turn. Tense: short fast sentences, close small details. Warm or sad: slower, objects, pauses. Funny: timing and a straight face. Cold: what is missing. Comedy never breaks a serious scene.
- ARMOR (story/bible/armor.md; invent nothing): whenever armor is worn, called or hit, show it. ARMOR-UP is a Garo-manner set piece of 150-300 words, slower than the fight around it: trigger, each plate with look, sound and temperature, helm last, first breath inside, first step. Then the weight and changed movement, which part takes the hit, the cost after (cold, Frost, the Tolls as heavy joints, never numbers). Armor-ups are aura moments: show how people nearby react. No Raiment in public unless canon says so.

## 10. BATTLES AND AURA
- Fights: setup (where, who, the plan in one line), then 3-5 clear beats in order (who moves, what it does, what it costs), then the result. Geography easy to draw. Terrain and preparation decide fights. Every fight shows one new, creative use of his power (battle-craft.md); never just a bigger weapon.
- Per arc roughly 30% flex, 35% puzzle, 25% strained, 10% costly. Aura moments are rare (2-4 per arc), earned by earlier hiding, and always have reaction shots (who goes silent, who kneels, what a veteran's hands do). Never narrate aura in adjectives alone.
- The circle fights at its true standing (power-system.md section 17): competent, working as a team, beaten only by out-of-spec threats, traps or by choice.
- No humiliate-and-reveal loops (at most once per arc, never the same shape). Politics: stakes, clock and rule stated once; every scene changes the situation (intrigue-craft.md).

## 11. NATURAL FLOW AND TEMPERAMENT (permanent)
- Nothing forced or rushed. Every power, reveal, armor, rule or twist is set up earlier and fits the scene; if not, delay or cut it. Before each scene ask: does this follow? Would a reader ask "why did nobody just...?" Fix the cause in the scene, not with an excuse line.
- Most characters control their emotions and act like normal people: calm reactions, ordinary reasons, no speeches, no random outbursts. Few are eccentric, each on purpose and with a reason in the bible.
- Avoid: characters acting stupid to keep the plot going, power-ups without cost, info dumps (max 3 sentences of pure exposition in a row), contrived coincidences, repeated misunderstandings, a reveal everyone saw coming but the hero. Max 1-2 new world concepts per chapter, shown through action.

## 12. READER MODEL
Teenagers and young adults reading web novels and light novels on a phone. Feel references (ideas of pace and clarity only; never copy): Holy Emperor's Grandson is a Necromancer, The Academy's Weapon Replicator, Mahouka, Bleach, Dandadan, Garo (armor scenes).
