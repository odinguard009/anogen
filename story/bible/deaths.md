# Deaths policy (reference)

- The main circle never dies: Eden, Lysette, Felix, Valerius, Seraphine, Rowan, Clara, Kirana, Han, Ishani, Duke Aldous, the Argentine household (Bertram, Greta, Pim, Odessa, Hammond), King Theodric, Ysolde, Ostwald, Okoru. They grow instead: new Tenet tiers, new roles, changed relationships.
- No death unless it carries clear meaning for plot or theme, is foreshadowed at least 10 chapters ahead, and is given a full scene of weight. Never kill for shock or to "raise stakes" cheaply. Nameless soldiers die in war, but the narration notices them.
- Tension comes from other costs: lost power (Candor's refusal 260-330), lost memory (Lysette, 420-445), broken trust (140, 214, 236), public cost (160, 290, 301-350), injury (Duke's fingers 200, Felix's arm 382), strained bonds, tested principles.
- "Feared dead but alive" is used once, fairly: Felix lost at sea (382), found alive (397); planted by Wardenshell's law and Thalos's tide. Clara's Veil replacement is not a death: she is rescued from the Hush (480).
- Already dead before the story: Liora (meaning: the root wound), Corentin Hollen (meaning: Lysette's grief and the Black Order's moral cost; cannot be saved).

## Planned deaths (complete list; add only with a purpose line)

2. Count Lucan Merrow, the Regent (ch 199): killed by the Tailor's trap to silence him; shows the Hush only uses humans. His grievance stays alive in the Ashen Crowns.
3. The Tailor (ch 200): Liora's killer, locked forever by Morrowpin and ended by Candor; closes the mother thread's first circle.
4. Lord Marshal Gideon Valdis (ch 247): holds the Teeth's inner gate; passes the Wall to Valerius; foreshadowed from ch 211 (his old wound, his letter to his daughter).
5. Empress Dragomira (ch 540): Vehra's host; dies refusing Vehra's last order, her one free act; shows the Binding's cruelty; frees Volkhovar to join the coalition. God-hosts may fall only with this weight; no other host dies.
- Han Shuzhen loses his arm to stone (cost, not death). Kell survives his god's ending.
6. Kaurveth (ch 640): given an ending that fulfils his want; not a slaughter.
7. The Tutor, the Emperor's last echo (ch 645): ends as he wished; the Emperor finally finishes.
