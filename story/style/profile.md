# Style profile (swappable; engine reads only this file for style)

## Voice
- First person, past tense. Dry, observant, understated humor; feelings shown through small actions rather than stated.
- Tactical narration in fights: read the terrain, name the options, pay the cost.

## Prose
- Concrete nouns and strong verbs; avoid stacked adjectives and filler adverbs.
- Vary sentence length. Short paragraphs in action; longer in quiet beats.
- Dialogue carries subtext; people rarely say exactly what they mean.

## Chapter shape
- Open in motion or with a concrete image, never a weather or waking-up cliche.
- End on a hook that is different in kind from the previous chapters' hooks.

## Freshness
- Do not reuse a simile, a gesture (sighing, clenching fists) or a sentence pattern more than once per arc.
- Each chapter: at least one surprising specific detail that belongs only to this world.

## Human feel (owner's priority: must not read as AI-written)
- Voice first: the narrator has opinions, dry asides, small unfair judgments. He notices odd, specific things a person would notice, not what a camera would.
- Uneven rhythm on purpose: fragments, a one-line paragraph, then a long run-on when the mind races. Never a steady, polished cadence.
- Do not explain a feeling after showing it. Do not end paragraphs or scenes with a tidy moral, summary or aphorism. Cut the last sentence if it only restates.
- No rule-of-three lists as a habit; no "not X, but Y" or "It wasn't X. It was Y." more than once per chapter; no stacked similes.
- Dialogue: people interrupt, dodge, answer a different question, trail off. Each speaker sounds different (vocabulary, length, verbal tics). Tags mostly "said"; action beats instead of adverbs.
- Contractions in narration and dialogue. Plain words over fancy ones. Concrete nouns (a chipped mug, a stale roll) over abstractions.
- Allow small mess: a joke that falls flat, a thought that wanders, a detail that matters later. Avoid symmetry between paragraphs and between chapters.
- Names and sentence openers vary; do not begin three sentences in a row with the protagonist's name or "He".
- Emotional peaks get restraint: fewer words, the body and objects carry it.
